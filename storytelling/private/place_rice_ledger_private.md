## Dialectic
a word gone subzero

## Revealed Thing & If/Then Implication
*Institution:* The Rice Ledger — an official record where harvest yields are inscribed in permanent ink, then never revised.  
*If/then logic (from thesis: heat is danger, coolness is virtue):*  
IF speech that carries emotional heat is suspect,  
AND records must remain neutral to be legitimate,  
THEN all verbal appeals about yield disputes must be cooled—stripped of tone, timing, context—before entering the ledger.  
→ Emotional truth cannot be archived; only its corpse can.

This is not corruption. It is protocol. The ledger does not lie—it *sublimates*. When a farmer says, “The flood took my field,” the clerk writes: “Yield reduced by natural variance.” Grief is edited into grammar.

## Episode Hooks
- A young farmer insists his father’s final words—“*not one grain for the state*”—be entered beside last season’s tally. The clerk refuses: “We record weight, not will.” Lek must decide whether to press the thumbprint anyway.
- Pom is seen copying entries at dusk, not into his notebook but onto loose rice-paper slips. One reads: “*jai yen khaeng met* — heart cold as tin.” The next day, the ledger’s lock is changed.

## Spatial Spots
1. **The Stamp Table** – where thumbprints are pressed and red seals dropped. Paper trembles here.
2. **The Glass Case** – holds the master ledger. Light fractures through its warped glass, bending numbers.
3. **The Cooling Bench** – outside, where petitioners wait ten minutes in silence before speaking. No one sits unless told.
