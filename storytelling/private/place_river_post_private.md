### Dialectic:  
**If honor is a face, then delivery is a performance.**  
Truth moves only when its arrival can be staged. The River Post exists because letters must not arrive unannounced — their landing must be timed, witnessed, softened. To hand over a letter at dawn is different from noon; to release it after rain implies delay was nature’s fault, not the sender’s neglect. The boy does not read, but he *knows* — by weight, by seal, by who brought it — what each envelope risks.

### Revealed Thing and Why It Is an If/Then of the Thesis:  
The timing and manner of delivery affect how truth lands. A reprimand handed at midday shames publicly; the same note slipped under a door at dusk becomes private concern. The institution enforces that *when* and *how* something is said alters its moral weight. Thus, the post isn’t neutral — it stages speech so faces may survive it.

### Episode Hooks:  
- A letter marked “return if undelivered” arrives addressed to Phum. The boy holds it three days before handing it over — long enough for rumor to shape the story behind it.  
- Pricha catches the boy altering the order of envelopes on the pole — switching a widow’s plea with a merchant’s invoice. When questioned, he says only: “She should not be first today.”  

### Noun Phrase:  
**delivery gone sideways**

### Spatial Spots:  
1. **The Clip Pole** – where sealed envelopes hang by brass hooks. Some sway, some are still — those pinned down arrived during argument.  
2. **The Boy’s Stool** – carved from temple scrapwood, one leg shorter than the others. He rocks it forward only when releasing mail.  
3. **The Wet Bench** – for opened letters left too long in rain. No one claims them. They dry slowly, ink bleeding names into blurs.
