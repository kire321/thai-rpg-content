### Dialectic:  
**If honor is a face, then repair is ritual concealment.**  
A broken mirror shows truth — jagged, multiplied — but showing it is violence. The mender does not restore clarity; she seals fracture so reflection becomes whole again, even if false. Her work says: *a face need not be real to be saved.* Mirrors are returned not to owners, but to the walls that held them — rehanging is part of the healing.

### Revealed Thing and Why It Is an If/Then of the Thesis:  
Faces must remain intact, even when damaged. The act of mending a mirror without removing cracks — covering them in metal breath — embodies the social imperative: heal appearance, not substance. The jars labeled *East*, *Southwest*, *Nightward* contain shards removed from noble homes; direction indicates whose face they once bore.

### Episode Hooks:  
- Phum brings a palm-sized mirror with a hairline split through the center. The mender covers it completely in silver leaf. That night, he uses it to reflect candlelight onto a letter while reading — casting the words across the ceiling, unseen by listeners below.  
- Bua requests a mirror rebuilt from two halves — one hers, one belonging to the dead dancer. The mender refuses: “One face, one vessel.” But she gives Bua a shard set in wax: “Hold this when you teach. She’ll see herself move.”

### Noun Phrase:  
**fracture sealed warm**

### Spatial Spots:  
1. **The Silver Breath Tray** – where leaf-thin metal lies exposed. The mender exhales over it to soften adhesion.  
2. **The Jar Wall** – seven clay vessels holding unmended shards. Dust inside shifts when certain names are spoken nearby.  
3. **The Unhung Rack** – holds repaired mirrors waiting return. None face inward — all reflect only sky or wall.
