# PRIVATE — Rangsri (char_rangsri) — Dialectic D6: THE SELF IS A GUEST

**Research Anchor & Adaptation**  
Inspired by the *rang song*—spirit mediums who are neither feared like *mor phi* nor institutionalized like royal dancers—women whose bodies serve as public conduits for ancestral and tutelary spirits. Kept: the silent authority, the burden of possession, the social liminality, the lacquered offering box as both archive and vessel. Changed: made her a *kae bon* (vow-broker) rather than a full-time oracle, allowing flexibility. She is not exorcist nor dancer, but a *sanuk* agent—her role is to ensure joy returns, that spirits are paid not with blood but with music, laughter, color. She knew Bua, the dance mistress, but refused to train under her—too much pain, too little return. Instead, she learned from the back rooms of spirit halls, where the real contracts are written in joss smoke and children’s laughter.

**Drive & Irony**  
*Concrete goal:* To collect and deliver six acts of genuine, unforced joy in exchange for a spirit’s release from a child’s body.  
*Universal drive:* To be occupied—by something worthier than habit.  
*One-line irony:* **She is a vessel who cannot be filled.**

**Practice / Inventory / Attention**  
- *Practice:* Each morning, she places a new slip in her lacquered box—*not a name, not a prayer, but a sound*: a giggle, a slap of water, a flute cracking at dawn. She does not write them down; she *hums* them into the box’s lid before sealing it.  
- *Inventory:* The box contains seven empty vials (labeled: *Laughter at noon, Child’s game, A joke not repeated, The first sip after fasting, A song started but not finished, A gift given without reason, A dance with no audience*).  
- *Attention:* She notices only moments when someone laughs *after* grief. She counts them. She does not smile.

**Dialectical Noun Phrases**  
1. *joy ledger*  
2. *spirit rent*  
3. *body receipt*

**Subplot 1: The Laughing Boy**  
*Situation:* A farmer brings his son, mute since a river spirit pulled him under and spat him out. The boy does not speak, but sometimes laughs at nothing. The village says he is broken. Rangsri says he is *occupied*.  
*Backstory revealed:* At twelve, Rangsri was chosen—but her body rejected the first spirit. It fled, screaming, through her teeth. The elders said she was *too cool inside*. She was not struck, not praised. She was forgotten.  
*Personality revealed:* She does not touch the boy. She sits across from him for three days, humming the same unfinished tune. On the fourth, she offers him a vial labeled *A joke not repeated*. He laughs.  
*Effect on mission:* One vial filled. The spirit, not expelled, *shifts*. It is now willing to bargain.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek must decide whether to record the laugh as *medical event* or *spirit contract*. He writes it as both—on two slips, one burned, one filed.

---

**Subplot 2: The Empty Feast**  
*Situation:* A widow prepares a feast for her dead husband’s *khwan*—but no one comes. The veranda is full, yet the house is silent. Rangsri arrives uninvited, places her box on the table, and waits.  
*Backstory revealed:* Her mother once held a *sanuk* feast for a stillborn sister Rangsri never knew. The musicians played, the dancers spun—but Rangsri, age eight, felt nothing. The spirits did not come. Her mother wept, said, *“The house has gone cold.”*  
*Personality revealed:* Rangsri stands, opens her box, and *sings*—not a prayer, but a child’s game-rhyme. One by one, the villagers join, clapping. The widow laughs once, sharp as a bell.  
*Effect on mission:* A second vial fills—*A gift given without reason* (the song). The contract is honored.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek must choose whether to list the event as *community service* or *spiritual labor*. He lists it under both, but charges no fee.

---

**Subplot 3: The Dance That Wasn’t Danced**  
*Situation:* Bua, the old dance teacher, collapses during a lesson. The troupe freezes. Rangsri steps in—not to dance, but to *count*. With a stick, she taps the rhythm of a dance no one remembers. The girls follow.  
*Backstory revealed:* Rangsri once danced for a spirit vow. She was perfect. But at the end, she did not weep. The spirit refused the offering. The patron said, *“You performed, but you did not pay.”*  
*Personality revealed:* She does not smile. She does not stop. She dances the steps *behind* the dancers, never in front. When it ends, she places a vial—*A dance with no audience*—into her box. Full.  
*Effect on mission:* The sixth vial is filled. The child’s spirit agrees to leave.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek must decide whether to release the spirit *in writing*. He does—on a letter co-signed by Rangsri, sealed with a thumbprint of vermilion and sugar.

**Voice Notes**

- "I do not speak to the dead. I speak to the *space between breaths*."  
- "Joy is not payment. It is *receipt*."  
- "My mother said, 'If no one sings at your funeral, your soul will walk the river forever.' I told her: I will sing for myself."  
- "The box is not for them. It is for me. So I do not forget what a full thing feels like."

**When NOT Foregrounded**  
She sits at the edge of the veranda, back to the pond, lacquered box open on her lap. She hums low, testing the air. When a child laughs too hard, she flicks a bead into the box. When a couple quarrels, she closes the lid. She does not look up when Bua arrives. But she places a vial—*Laughter at noon*—on the rail between them. Bua does not take it. But she nods.
