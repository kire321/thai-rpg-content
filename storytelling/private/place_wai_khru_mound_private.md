# Private Narrative Plans — The Wai Khru Mound

The Wai Khru Mound reveals the D3 if/then: *If gratitude is a debt, then every teacher is a creditor, and every ceremony a repayment schedule.* Here, obligation is not metaphor — it is calendared, spatialized, enforced by the body’s posture. The mound is where debt becomes visible: the younger the student, the lower the wai; the deeper the debt, the longer the incense burns.

**Episode hooks:**
- A mother insists her son write an apology letter to his dance teacher *before* the wai khru, because “you cannot repay a debt while standing on bad words.”
- The district officer arrives uninvited during the ceremony, clipboard in hand, to document who owes what to whom — “for district records,” he says, “not judgment.”
- Wan is asked to write a false letter claiming a dead boy attended the mound last year — his parents need proof of repayment so merit will pass to him in the next life.

**Dialectical noun phrase:**  
"a repayment schedule written in incense smoke"

**Named spatial spots:**
1. The Pen Stone – chipped granite where the ceremonial pen is tapped
2. The Creditor’s Seat – a low stool reserved for the teacher, never moved
3. The Ash Ring – blackened earth where burned offerings sink into root-tangle
4. The Debt Arches – two frangipani trunks forming a natural sala
5. The Child’s Kneeler – worn sandstone where bare knees leave dust-trails
