## Dialectic
a grief run to fever

## Revealed Thing & If/Then Implication
*Institution:* The Charcoal Hearth — where all cooking fires are smothered nightly, leaving only controlled embers. Full flame is forbidden after dusk; it draws moths, officials, memory.  
*If/then logic (from thesis: fever = moral weather; heat must be contained):*  
IF visible flame signals loss of control,  
AND public grief risks collective overheating,  
THEN mourning must be conducted at ember-temperature—slow, indirect, unwatched.  
→ To cook in the dark is to grieve without spectacle.

Women speak of “feeding the coals” when a child dies. They do not say “burning.”

## Episode Hooks
- A widow stirs the hearth all night, refusing to cover the coals. Her daughter pleads with Lek: “She’ll get sick.” But she says nothing—just feeds green wood, making smoke, not heat. The district officer threatens to revoke her fuel ration.
- Pom arrives just before dawn. He places a folded paper into the pit, does not push it down. It chars slowly. No one asks what it says.

## Spatial Spots
1. **The Ash Lid** – a woven mat used to smother the pit. Removed only during feeding.
2. **The Rod Rack** – iron prods marked with nicks for each family. One has a chipped tip—broke during a storm fire.
3. **The Waiting Stones** – three flat rocks where women sit while coals stabilize. Silence is expected.
