# PRIVATE — The Rice Counter

**Dialectic**  
*Thesis implication:* If the soul is a guest, then hunger is not lack but eviction notice — a body without food has no tenant, and no spirit will enter an empty house. Thus, every grain measured is also a name recalled: to feed the living is to rehouse the wandering dead.

**Revealed thing**  
The village’s granary system doubles as a soul registry. Farmers who contribute rice earn tokens that double as votive tags — their ancestors’ names inscribed on slips tucked beneath sacks. When a portion is distributed, the recipient must speak one name aloud. Silence voids the claim. This is not charity; it is tenancy verification.

**Episode hooks**  
- A sack arrives marked with Rangsri’s mother’s name — unregistered, unclaimed in decades. No one speaks it. The rice spoils within hours.  
- A child distributes rations alone. He says no names. The elders argue: is he *khwan*-less, or a new kind of host?

**Noun phrase**  
body receipt

**Spatial spots**  
1. The Weighing Post — where sacks are hoisted and bells rung  
2. Ledger Stand — the open book under glass, guarded by ants in oil moats  
3. Name Bench — where recipients sit before speaking, mouths rinsed with salt water
