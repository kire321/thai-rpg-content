### Dialectic:  
**If honor is a face, then value is measured in silence.**  
Salt is currency here, but not money — it carries no name, leaves no record. The store operates because debts paid in salt cannot be traced. To accept salt is to say: *I know what this means, but I will not speak it.* The bins sort shame: white for minor slights, grey for family disputes, pink — rare, imported — for royal missteps.

### Revealed Thing and Why It Is an If/Then of the Thesis:  
When truth costs face, compensation flows without declaration. The salt store formalizes unacknowledged restitution: a man whose son slandered another pays in grey salt; the injured party collects it later as if routine. No confession occurs, yet balance is restored. The ledger tracks only volume, never names — proof that accounting can exist without accusation.

### Episode Hooks:  
- Phum exchanges a small cloth bundle for two scoops of pink salt. The clerk doesn’t look up. Later, Wan sees him leave a folded paper in the temple donation slit.  
- Pricha discovers the red-chalk ledger has tiny mirror shards embedded in certain pages — reflecting light only at dusk. He realizes someone is marking entries meant to be seen *only* when half-hidden.

### Noun Phrase:  
**debt held in grain**

### Spatial Spots:  
1. **The White Bin** – fullest, refilled weekly. Used for late payments, missed greetings, forgotten birthdays.  
2. **The Chalk Table** – where women trace amounts in red before scooping. Mistakes are left — corrections would draw eyes.  
3. **The Hollow Step** – beneath the back stair, loose since last flood. Holds unsigned receipts, rotting slowly.
