# char_mor_duang — Narrative Blueprint

**Research anchor & adaptation**:  
Pridi Banomyong is transmuted into Kamnan Noi—a man who studied under royal astronomers in Bangkok but returned to his canal village after the 1932 upheaval left him politically exposed. Like Pridi, he sought rational order through law and celestial logic, but unlike the statesman, he carries no mandate—only ledgers. The *mor duang* trade is preserved as licensed readers of fate, operating under the Ministry of Religious Affairs’ obscure Bureau of Celestial Timekeeping. Their charts govern land transfers, court adjournments, and coronation dates. I kept the irony: he can read the sky’s verdicts but cannot alter his own descent. Changed: removed exile; instead, he *never leaves*, bound by a vow to witness the reign’s end from the exact latitude of his birth.

---

**Universal drive**: *To be the author of an unbroken record.*  
Underneath: To inscribe a truth so precise it cannot be denied—even by power. Not justice, not redemption, but *recording* as resistance.

**Structural irony (one body, both poles)**:  
*The only person who believes the chart is absolute—who also knows every chart is a lie.*

---

**Practice / Inventory / Attention**:  
- **Practice**: At dawn, he recalibrates his pocket chronometer using star sightings, though electricity has come to the village.  
- **Inventory**: Keeps three ledgers: one public (events scheduled), one private (actual occurrences), one sealed (birth-chart annotations in disappearing ink).  
- **Attention**: Notices when people arrive late or early by *exact minutes*, muttering corrections under his breath. Records rainfall start times to the second.

---

### Noun Phrases:

## Subplot 1: The Widow’s Eclipse  
**Situation**: A widow demands a funeral date changed—her husband died at 4:07 AM, not 4:13 as recorded. She needs the earlier time to inherit land before the district officer audits. Kamnan refuses.  

**Backstory revealed**: In Bangkok, he corrected a prince’s birth minute; the court astronomer burned the amendment. He fled, knowing truth without authority is suicide.  

**Personality revealed**: His rigidity isn’t piety—it’s survival. He won’t rewrite time, even for mercy. But he slips her a paper: the *true* moon phase, implying a different heir.  

**Effect on mission**: The dispute escalates, pulling in the pawnshop appraiser, who owns part of the deed. The PC must mediate written time versus felt time.  

**PC’s decision**: Lek must file the monk’s version or the widow’s. Kamnan says nothing—hands Lek the sealed ledger. Lek doesn’t open it. Files the monk’s time. The land passes legally. Later, the widow burns her house down “by accident.” Kamnan records the fire’s start: 3:58 AM. *Chart gone blind.*

---

## Subplot 2: The Boatboy’s Name  
**Situation**: The boatman wants his son named at a water-blessing, but Kamnan vetoes it—the child’s birth aligns with Rahu’s shadow. A name given now will “feed the eclipse.” The mother insists: it’s already carved on the prow.  

**Backstory revealed**: Kamnan’s own son drowned at six—named under a cracked hour. Born at 5:52:17. Died at 5:52:33. He’d begged to delay the naming; the monk refused.  

**Personality revealed**: He fears not spirits, but precision. The universe counts seconds. Humans lie. He gives the mother a new name in sidereal code—only valid at moon-rise.  

**Effect on mission**: The blessing becomes a silent standoff. The district officer arrives, mocks “superstition,” demands registration. Delay means losing rice subsidies.  

**PC’s decision**: Lek must register the name. He chooses the one on the prow. Kamnan watches, expressionless. That night, he draws two charts: one under the wrong name, one under the true. Both end in water. He burns the second. *Time still burning.*

---

## Subplot 3: The Reign’s Last Breath  
**Situation**: Rumors spread: the King is dying. The district officer asks Kamnan to cast the *duang muang*—the city’s succession chart. If the sky favors a republic, he could be arrested. If he falsifies it, the rains may fail.  

**Backstory revealed**: Kamnan once drafted a constitutional almanac—linking laws to lunar phases—for Pridi’s government. It was burned on March 24, 1947, at 6:18 PM. Thunder cracked as the archive ignited.  

**Personality revealed**: He believes the state is a living horoscope. Its death-throes are legible. But speaking them is treason. He begins casting the chart—not for the officer, but for the veranda. Secretly teaches Lek the symbols.  

**Effect on mission**: The monk overhears, reports him. The boatman hides the brass astrolabe. The pawnshop appraiser offers to “safeguard” the ledgers—a threat.  

**PC’s decision**: Lek is asked to witness Kamnan’s “voluntary retirement.” He can sign, denying knowledge, or refuse—and risk being branded a rebel. He signs. Kamnan hands him the key to the sealed ledger. Says only: “Open it when the king dies.” Lek pockets it. Kamnan walks to the riverbank. Watches the sky. Does not return. *Sky turned paper.*

## When NOT foregrounded:
Sits cross-legged at the edge of the veranda, adjusting his compass to the sun’s angle. Records the arrival time of each visitor in a small ledger. If someone speaks of “luck” or “chance,” he corrects the minute—quietly, not unkindly. Never looks up unless a clock strikes uneven.
