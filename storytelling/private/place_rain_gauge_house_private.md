### Dialectic  
A LIFE IS A CHART — (c) timing is the only freedom: you cannot change what, only when.  

### The revealed thing and why it is an if/then of the thesis  
**If** a life is a fixed sequence beginning at birth, **then** all communal rhythms must also begin at a recorded second. The Rain Gauge House enforces this: the first drop of the monsoon must be *seen entering the tube*, not inferred. The planting season, tax cycles, and temple fairs hinge on this single observation. The institution reveals that nature’s cycles are not felt or guessed—they are timestamped. The gauge is never automated; human witness is required, because only a witness can lie—and thus, must be held accountable for the truth.  

### Episode hooks  
- The gauge overflows during the night. The official insists it began at 2:14 AM; three farmers swear it was 1:58. Lek is asked to verify the ledger. Kamnan arrives, checks his chronometer, says nothing.  
- A child climbs the shed to drop a pebble into the tube, faking the start. The act is both sacrilege and salvation—her family needs to plant early to survive.
