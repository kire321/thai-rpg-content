## Dialectic
a letter gone cold

## Revealed Thing & If/Then Implication
*Institution:* The Grammar Mill — where written language must undergo a 12-hour cooling period before being printed. Any notice, plea, or announcement must sit in the cooling tray, exposed to night air, so “heat may rise off it.”  
*If/then logic (from thesis: composure is public utility):*  
IF words carry temperature,  
AND hot words ignite unrest,  
THEN all public text must be allowed to cool before becoming real.  
→ Delay is not inefficiency. It is quarantine.

The mill does not censor content. It trusts time to leach urgency from syntax. A cry for justice becomes “a matter under observation.” A death threat becomes “an expression of concern.”

## Episode Hooks
- A student sets type for a protest notice: “We will not pay.” At dawn, the cooling tray is empty. The type is scattered. Someone re-set it as: “We seek clarification.” Lek must trace who touched it.
- Pom visits weekly. He doesn’t print. He removes one cooled phrase each time, folds it, and pockets it. Never looks at what he takes.

## Spatial Spots
1. **The Type Floor** – wooden trays labeled by syllable shape. Tones sorted by height of cast.
2. **The Cooling Tray** – elevated mesh rack. Paper left here yellows slightly at edges.
3. **The Crank Well** – where the press is turned. Requires two people: one to feed paper, one to watch the clock.
