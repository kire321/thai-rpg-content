# PRIVATE — Phra Kam (char_phra_kam) — Dialectic D2: GOODNESS IS A LEDGER

**Research Anchor & Adaptation**  
Inspired by Buddhadasa Bhikkhu and Ajahn Mun Bhuridatta: fused into a single fictional monk, Phra Kam ("Virtue Ledger"), whose name mocks the system he resists. Kept: the critique of merit-as-currency, the tension between forest asceticism and institutional Sangha, the irony of becoming a holy figure despite rejecting veneration. Changed: merged timelines, compressed geography, invented personal history to serve the veranda’s economy of desire. Ajahn Mun’s wandering and Buddhadasa’s metaphorical dhamma are both present — but Phra Kam is no reformer, no teacher. He is a man who stopped believing and cannot leave.

**Drive & Irony**  
*Concrete Goal:* To complete 365 days of silent meditation beside the veranda pond, unmoved by alms or appeal.  
*Universal Drive beneath:* To prove that goodness does not accumulate.  
*Irony (one body, both poles):* A monk who keeps a ledger of virtues he refuses to count.

**Practice / Inventory / Attention**  
- *Practice:* Each dawn, he opens a small black notebook and writes one word: either “received” or “refused.” Never “given.”  
- *Inventory:* The notebook, a rusted spoon (his only eating tool), a threadbare robe with no donor name stitched on the hem.  
- *Attention:* Notices only what is offered — and how it is offered. Sees the tremor in a hand extending rice, the pride in a woman placing gold leaf on the tray.

**Three Dialectical Noun Phrases**  
1. *Merit ledger gone sour*  
2. *Donation bowl with a hunger*  
3. *Alms rice gone brackish*

---

**Subplot 1: The Unfed Bowl**  
*Situation:* A wealthy widow arrives to make a grand donation, insisting Phra Kam accept a gilded alms bowl. He refuses. She threatens to withdraw support from the temple school.  
*Backstory revealed:* As a young monk in Bangkok, Phra Kam passed every Pali exam — but failed the test of silence. He spoke out when a rich man bought a whole temple hall’s merit, erasing his son’s murder debt. His superiors silenced him. He fled north.  
*Personality revealed:* His silence is not humility. It is defiance. He will not be a bank teller for blood money.  
*Effect on mission:* The school’s funds are at risk. Lek must mediate.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek proposes a compromise: the bowl is placed at the edge of the pond, filled with water — not rice. It becomes a mirror. The widow sees her face, not her piety. She leaves quietly. Phra Kam writes: “refused.”

---

**Subplot 2: The Debt of Dust**  
*Situation:* Sangwan brings the jar of unclaimed bone-ash — the dancer with no kin. He asks if Phra Kam can chant for her. The monk refuses: no name, no merit.  
*Backstory revealed:* Phra Kam once oversaw a mass ordination where 200 boys became novices — all sons of poor families selling virtue. One boy died of fever the next day. His parents demanded their deposit back. The abbot said merit is non-refundable.  
*Personality revealed:* He believes merit is theft when kin are absent. The ledger demands a name. Without one, the soul floats, unpaid.  
*Effect on mission:* The village fears the ash will bring bad luck. Lek must decide: invent a lineage, or let the dancer vanish.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek writes a false letter, claiming the dancer was Sangwan’s cousin. Phra Kam sees it — says nothing. That night, he chants one short sutra. Writes in ledger: “received. (Unasked.)”

---

**Subplot 3: The Weight of a Spoon**  
*Situation:* The district officer accuses Phra Kam of vagrancy. He has no temple affiliation, no papers. The boatman offers to smuggle him away.  
*Backstory revealed:* Phra Kam was once a state-certified monk. He burned his certificate after seeing a forest ascetic — Ajahn Mun’s disciple — jailed for wandering. He took the man’s spoon and walked south.  
*Personality revealed:* He carries the spoon not as relic, but as evidence: a tool that feeds only when offered, yet weighs more than gold.  
*Effect on mission:* The officer demands Lek vouch for him. Doing so risks Lek’s clerkship.  
*PC’s decision:* Lek declares Phra Kam “ward of the veranda” — not a monk, not a criminal, but a guest. The officer laughs. The boatman nods. Phra Kam eats from the spoon that night. Writes: “refused. (Again.)”

**Voice Notes**  
- “Merit is not virtue. It is memory — of who gave, and who took.”  
- “When a child hands me rice, I see her mother’s hands first. Then the ledger. Then nothing.”  
- “I do not save souls. I watch them pass. Like leaves on water. Some sink fast. Some float for years.”

**When NOT Foregrounded (2-3 lines):**  
Sits by the pond, back to the scribe-hut. Counts breaths, not beads. If someone offers rice, he stares at their hand until they withdraw it. Once, he caught a falling lotus petal midair — then let it drop.
