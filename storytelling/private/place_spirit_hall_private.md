# PRIVATE — The Spirit Hall

**Dialectic**  
*Thesis implication:* If the soul is a guest, then promises are leases — signed not in blood but in breath, paid in performance. Spirits do not haunt; they *hold deposits*. Break the terms, and the rent comes due in laughter, music, or sudden silence.

**Revealed thing**  
The hall brokers temporary possessions: a farmer rents courage for harvest; a bride loans joy for seven days. Payment is a future act — a song sung at midnight, a dance performed blindfolded. Default means the spirit keeps the down payment: a memory, a habit, a tone of voice.

**Episode hooks**  
- Rangsri retrieves a vial labeled *A joke not repeated* from Shelf B-7 — last seen in her box weeks ago. She does not explain.  
- A man demands his rented grief back — he borrowed it to mourn properly, but now cannot stop. The clerk shrugs: “You used it early. It expired.”

**Noun phrase**  
spirit rent

**Spatial spots**  
1. Loan Window — where terms are whispered, fingers pressed to paper  
2. Shelf Rows — categorized by emotion: Courage, Grief, Laughter, Patience  
3. Back Room — inkwells steam; pens write themselves when the air cools
