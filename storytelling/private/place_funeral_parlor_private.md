### Dialectic  
A LIFE IS A CHART — (a) every event has a timestamp in a book already bound; effort arranges furniture in a built house.  

### The revealed thing and why it is an if/then of the thesis  
**If** a life is a star-map fixed at birth, **then** death must conform to the same ledger. The Funeral Parlor does not schedule burials by availability—it retrofits the death into the *auspicious window* of the chart. If someone dies at an inauspicious minute, the time is adjusted on paper. The clock behind the counter runs slow by design: it allows the timekeeper to “catch up” to the correct moment. The institution reveals that death is not an end but a *misalignment* to be corrected. Truth is not in the body, but in the record.  

### Episode hooks  
- A widow demands her husband’s death be logged at 4:07, not 4:13. The timekeeper complies—but Kamnan later visits, checks the ledger, and records the true time in his private book.  
- The district officer arrives to audit burial logs. He brings a new electric clock. The timekeeper refuses to connect it: “It ticks too fast for the dead.”  

### Noun phrase  
**chart gone blind**

### Spatial spots  
1. **The Red-Black Ledger** – open on the counter, entries corrected without erasure.  
2. **The Coffin Rack** – unfinished pine boxes wait for their owner’s *right* death time.  
3. **The Silent Bell** – a brass gong that rings only when the time is *officially* set.
