# char_phum — Narrative Design Dossier

**Research anchor & adaptation**:  
Inspired by Dan Beach Bradley and Sunthorn Phu — the foreign printer silenced by the king he served, and the poet ruined by speaking truth to power. From Bradley: the fate of a man who introduced truthful public record into a world where truth must bend to save face. From Sunthorn Phu: the artist whose words were currency but whose tongue bankrupted him. Kept: the printer’s exile from the press, the poet’s gift for indirection, the shared wound of being punished for clarity. Changed: fused into a native Siamese man, mid-career civil scribe, fallen from the Bangkok printing bureau after reading a letter aloud that named a noble’s illegitimate son. Not exiled, but “returned to roots” — a polite expulsion. Fictionalized as “the Fifth Reign’s clean-up,” never named.

**Universal drive under goal**:  
*To be heard without consequence.*  
Concrete goal: To read every letter brought to the veranda in a voice that carries the truth but leaves no scar. The drive manifests not as rebellion, but as containment — to speak fully and yet leave no wound, to deliver fact without fracture.

**Structural irony (one body, both poles)**:  
*A man whose job is to give voice, who has lost his own face for speaking.*

---

### Practice / Inventory / Attention:
- **Practice**: Repairs broken spectacles with brass wire, fish-glue, and a magnifying lens salvaged from the old print office. Never wears them himself.
- **Inventory**: Keeps a hidden ledger of “unsaid lines” — phrases from letters he read aloud but altered to spare someone’s standing. Each entry marked with a tiny mirror shard glued beside it.
- **Attention**: Notices micro-reactions — a twitch at a name, a blink when a date is misread. Tracks who flinches when which word is spoken.

1. **voice gone brittle**

### Subplot 1: *The District Officer’s Letter*

**Situation**:  
The district officer brings a formal reprimand for Sangwan, accusing him of forging remittance letters. He demands it be read aloud at the veranda meeting. Pricha hesitates — the accusation will ruin Wan.

**Backstory revealed**:  
Phum recognizes the officer’s script — he wrote this same man’s love letter two years prior, one that nearly caused a duel when read too plainly. He had softened it then. Now, the officer wants blood, not balance.

**Personality revealed**:  
Phum offers to read the letter — but alters three key lines: “forgery” becomes “unauthorized penmanship,” “deceit” becomes “private devotion,” “prosecute” becomes “review.” His voice stays level, syllables polished like river stones.

**Effect on mission**:  
The accusation lands, but not fatally. Wan survives the day — but the officer knows he was lied to. He begins watching Phum.

**PC’s decision**:  
Pricha must decide whether to record the *altered* version in the village book. He does — but writes in the margin: *“Read with Phum’s tongue.”* A silent acknowledgment: truth here is layered, not false.

### Subplot 2: *The Monk’s Unclaimed Ashes*

**Situation**:  
The monk brings a small urn — dancer’s ashes, unclaimed. Bua the dance teacher cannot take them; she says the dead girl’s family would lose face if it were known she danced after ordination. The urn must be placed quietly.

**Backstory revealed**:  
Phum once set a poem about a ghost-dancer in the *Bangkok Recorder*. It was praised — until someone recognized the subject as a living courtesan’s daughter. The fallout cost him his post. He hasn’t written since.

**Personality revealed**:  
He volunteers to carry the urn to the riverside at dawn. At the bank, he recites a short verse — not Buddhist, not official, but a fragment of an old lullaby dancers used to sing. No one hears but the water.

**Effect on mission**:  
Wan hears of this and begins to trust Phum — sees in him another keeper of buried things. He later slips Phum a sealed envelope: “For if I don’t come back.”

**PC’s decision**:  
Pricha must log the burial. He records only: *“Ashes released, no name taken.”* But he gives the urn’s number to Phum — an unspoken transfer of custodianship over what must not be said.

### Subplot 3: *The Photograph That Wouldn’t Develop*

**Situation**:  
A widow brings a wet, crumpled photograph — her son in uniform, last image before he vanished in the south. She wants it read, as if it were a letter. No one knows how.

**Backstory revealed**:  
Phum once managed the palace photo registry. He learned to “read” faces in negatives — not just who, but *whose*. He also learned to burn plates when ordered. He still dreams of blank film curling in flame.

**Personality revealed**:  
He dries the photo by the stove, studies it with his lens. Says: “He kept good company. Look — the officer beside him wears the southern star. That means he wasn’t alone when he fell.” It’s not certain. But it’s kind.

**Effect on mission**:  
Word spreads: Phum can “read” more than paper. Villagers begin bringing objects — locks of hair, torn tickets, even a child’s shoe. He listens, nods, speaks only what can be borne.

**PC’s decision**:  
Pricha considers banning such requests — they aren’t clerical work. But seeing the widow weep *less*, he allows it. He starts a new ledger: *“Things Brought to Be Understood.”* First entry: *“Photo, Phum spoke for it.”*

### Voice Notes:
- Speaks in low, even tones. Never raises pitch. Uses formal register even with children.
- Pauses half a second before answering — not from doubt, but from calculation.
- When refusing to speak, doesn’t stay silent — instead says something true but incomplete. Example: asked if he knew a missing boy, replies: *“I knew his handwriting.”*
- Rarely uses pronouns. Prefers titles: “the younger sister,” “the one who rows at dawn.”

---

### Behavior when NOT foregrounded:
Sits at the edge of the veranda, repairing spectacles or cleaning his lens. Listens to every word spoken, tracking shifts in tone. Occasionally hands someone a pair of mended glasses — not because they need them, but because they’ve been squinting at a letter. If a visitor speaks too bluntly, he clears his throat once — a soft chime of warning.
