### If/Then Implication of the Recurring Character's Contradiction:  
*A keeper of unspoken debts who tallies without naming — just as Phum records altered truths in his hidden ledger, she logs restitution that denies its own existence.* Both maintain systems that acknowledge wrongs while erasing blame.

### Recurring Function:  
The party exchanges official tokens for salt when settling disputes discreetly. They also consult her ledger (indirectly) to trace hidden obligations among villagers.

### Episode Hooks:  
- She accepts a pinch of ash mixed with salt from Phum — logs it as “one measure, west bin.” Days later, Wan finds the urn from the monk’s subplot buried beneath that section.  
- When Pricha tries to record a salt transaction in the village book, she slides him a red-chalk nub. He realizes: writing it in ink invalidates it. Must use her tools, her hand, her silence.

### Noun Phrases:  
**tally gone voiceless**  
**weight known blind**  
**balance kept red**

### Voice Notes:  
- Speaks in formulae only: “Accepted. Volume: two scoops. Bin: grey.” Deviation causes pause, re-scoop.  
- Refers to people by contribution: “the rice-giver,” “the debt-bringer,” never kinship or title.  
- If asked about a past entry, stares at the spot where her hand last moved — as if memory lives in muscle, not mind.
