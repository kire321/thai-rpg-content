# PRIVATE — The Caretaker of the Mound (char_wai_khru_caretaker) — Dialectic D3: LOVE IS A DEBT

The Caretaker embodies a smaller, generic instance of Sangwan’s contradiction: *She maintains the records of repayment so meticulously that no one needs to remember — yet her own son never came to the mound, and she cannot say whether he is debtor or default.* Her function is to witness and tally: the party needs her to confirm whether a letter was truly sent, whether a pen was tapped, whether a name was spoken in the smoke.

**Recurring function:** She verifies debts. The party consults her when a parent denies an offering, a student claims unfair punishment, or a teacher is accused of withholding merit. She never judges — only recites what was burned, what was left, what name was whispered.

**Episode hooks:**
- She quietly hands Lek a scrap of paper with Wan’s sister’s name on it — “left in the ashes last year, never claimed.” It matches Wan’s handwriting.
- When Mae arrives, the Caretaker asks her: “Did your daughter ever tap the Pen Stone?” — unknowingly threatening the silver letters.
- She begins leaving unlit incense at the mound for “the hand that writes the letters no one sees” — a ritual without a name, a debt without a debtor.

**Dialectical noun phrases:**
- "an unclaimed offering in the ash ring"
- "a creditor who sweeps the debt into trees"
- "a repayment no one remembers making"

**Voice notes:**
Speaks in receipts. “Three sticks for Teacher Boon, left after noon. One betel, unburned, taken back by the mother.” Refers to people by their debt-status: “the one who defaulted,” “the one who overpaid,” “the one who never knelt.” When asked about her own son, she turns and sweeps ash over ash — the only lie she allows herself. Calls the frangipani “the ledger-trees.” Drinks nothing but boiled water from the pond — “what passes through clean, stays clean.”
