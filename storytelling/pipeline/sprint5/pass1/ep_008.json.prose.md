## Act 1 — The Naming Dispute
PLACE: The School Veranda
The Boatman's wife: [presses her forehead to the veranda floorboards, her voice cracking] "The name is already cut into the wood."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi does not look up from his ledger, his finger resting on a column of numbers.
NARRATOR: The boatman stands behind his wife, his hands clenched around a length of rope.
The Boatman's wife: [looks up, eyes desperate] "We need this name blessed, or we lose the rice subsidy and our boat."
The Monk: [clears his throat] "The blessing can proceed, but the naming is a separate matter."
Kamnan Noi: [closes his ledger with a soft snap] "The child was born at 5:52:17."
Lek: [leans forward] "What is the problem with that exact minute?"
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [enters from the back room, polishing a small brass weight] "A name given under a shadow won't hold."
Kamnan Noi: [opens the ledger again, points to a line of script]
The Boatman's wife: [shows Lek her palms, raw from sanding the boat's prow]
Kamnan Noi: "The ceremony is at dawn; the district officer arrives at seven to sign the subsidy papers."
The Type Monitor: [steps in from the street, adjusting his glasses] "They could just use a different name for the register."
Kamnan Noi: [shakes his head once]
NARRATOR: The monk strains to hear the whispered argument from the next room, the words muffled by the wooden wall.
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [leans close] "How good is the boy's father at steering through trouble?" [[tag_045]]
The Boatman's wife: [looks at her raw palms, manages a small smile] "Her mood lifting for a moment." [[tag_131]]
The Type Monitor: [offers her a clean cloth] "Good night's rest might help those hands."
NARRATOR: The pawnshop appraiser's brass weight is one of three in his pocket: one for letters, one for salt, one for names. He touches each as he speaks.
The Boatman's wife: [whispers the carved name into the floorboard grain] "A name must land softly."

## Act 2 — Salt and Water
PLACE: The River Post
NARRATOR: At the river post, the new boat sits in the water, a cloth draped over the carved name on the prow.
NARRATOR: The district officer checks his wristwatch and taps his foot on the wooden platform.
The Boatman: [offers a cup of tea to the officer] "Tea?"
The District Officer: [waves it away] "No."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi stands apart, adjusting a brass compass against the horizon.
NARRATOR: The salt clerk arrives with a sack of coarse salt for the blessing.
The District Officer: [turns to Lek] "Is the paperwork in order?"
Lek: [opens his tin box, lays out the birth record and the boat registration] "Here."
Kamnan Noi: [walks over, places his sealed ledger on top of the registration form]
The Boatman's wife: [pulls the cloth from the prow, revealing the carved name]
The District Officer: [leans in to read it, then looks at Kamnan Noi]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi says nothing, only watches the water.
NARRATOR: The monk begins chanting, and the salt clerk pours a line of white grains along the gunwale.
The Boatman's wife: [reaches out as if to touch the carved letters]
The Boatman: [gently stops her hand] "Stop."
The District Officer: [nods] "Stop. The name is already there, and I know the rules." [[tag_209]]
The Salt Clerk: "This salt is for the boat, not the name."
NARRATOR: The monk pauses his chant, listening to the distant rumble of thunder.
The Salt Clerk: [looks at the sky] "We should wait for the rain to stop before we finish." [[tag_269]]
NARRATOR: The line of salt on the gunwale does not scatter in the breeze; each grain holds its place as if magnetized.
NARRATOR: The monk's chant hangs over the water, a delivery suspended between river and sky.
