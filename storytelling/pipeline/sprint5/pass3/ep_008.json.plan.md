## STICKY SITUATION
The District Officer has posted a new, mandatory schedule for salt collection at the River Post, tied to a census. Kamnan Noi declares the schedule’s posted time is inauspicious and refuses to let the boatman collect his salt, leaving the boatman unable to pay his mooring tax by sunset.

### Act 2

2. The oilskin wrapping one corner has torn, showing an earlier draft date underneath.

5. The boatman’s young son is there, holding the mooring line of their empty boat.

### Act 3
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The District Officer, The Boatman, The Boatman’s son
1. The District Officer steps onto the platform, his boots loud on the wood.
2. He asks Lek, as the temple clerk, to confirm the schedule was posted and visible this morning.
3. Before Lek can answer, Kamnan Noi states the precise minute of posting.
4. The District Officer laughs and calls it village superstition.
5. He says the law cares about the posted hour, not the stars, and the salt is now state-held for tardiness.
6. The boatman pleads, showing his tax notice.
7. The District Officer offers a solution: the boatman can pay a fine instead, using the salt’s value as collateral.
8. He looks at Kamnan Noi and adds that astrologers who disrupt commerce can be re-assigned.
9. Kamnan Noi does not react; he writes in his ledger.
10. The boatman’s son starts to cry quietly.
11. The District Officer turns to Lek and tells him to sign a witness statement about the posting time.
12. He places the paper and a pen on the schedule itself, using it as a desk.
13. DEADLINE CONVERGES: The sun touches the horizon; the tax collector’s boat is visible down the canal.
14. DECISION — dilemma line (The District Officer): "Clerk, your signature settles this. ### Act 4
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Boatman, The Salt Clerk, The Pawnshop Appraiser
1. Back on the veranda, the Salt Clerk has brought the boatman’s portion of salt in a small sack.
2. The Pawnshop Appraiser counts coins into the boatman’s hand, not reaching the full tax amount.
3. The boatman gives the coins to his son to take to the tax boat.
4. Kamnan Noi sits at the edge, observing the chronometer in his palm.
5. THESIS "delivery gone sideways": Lek does not sign the District Officer’s paper; he wrote a separate note citing the visible draft date and gave it to the officer instead.
6. The Salt Clerk says the District Officer left, taking the schedule with him.
7. Kamnan Noi hands the boatman a small, folded slip from his private ledger.
8. The boatman opens it; it contains a string of numbers and a single word: “tomorrow.”
9. The boatman looks at Kamnan Noi, who gives no explanation.
10. The Pawnshop Appraiser asks what the paper says.
11. The boatman crumples the slip and throws it into the pond.
12. Kamnan Noi records the exact time the paper hits the water.
