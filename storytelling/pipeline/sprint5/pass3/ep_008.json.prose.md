## Act 1 — The Salt Chit Unclaimed
PLACE: the school veranda
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk explains the schedule again from memory.
Kamnan Noi: [without looking up from his ledger] "The collection window opened seventeen minutes before the official posting."
NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser steps from the back of the shop, polishing a small scale.
NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser examines the salt chit with interest; Lek reads the schedule aloud — it lists the boatman’s name and a three-hour window starting at nine.
Kamnan Noi: [taps his chronometer] "The official notice was hammered into the post at nine-seventeen."
The Salt Clerk: [gestures toward the store] "The salt is measured and waiting in its bin, but the store is closed until the next window tomorrow, please." [[tag_161]]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi closes his public ledger and opens his private one; he makes a single mark in the private ledger, then slides a thin, sealed envelope from between its pages and tucks it into his shirt.
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [to the boatman] "I’ll buy your chit at half value, right now."
The Boatman: [turns to Lek] "Go to the River Post and witness the time on the notice — the coming hour is all we have." [[tag_045]]
NARRATOR: The new schedule from the District Officer arrived at dawn, nailed to the River Post with a tax stamp, and by mid-morning the boatman’s salt chit was the only one left unclaimed.
NARRATOR: The Delivery Boy arrives on the veranda, panting, holding a folded copy of the schedule.
The Boatman: [stands on the veranda, holding his stamped chit] "I need that salt to sell before sunset, or the tax collector takes my boat."

## Act 2 — Shadow and Salt
PLACE: The River Post
NARRATOR: At the River Post, the nailed schedule is already warping in the sun.
NARRATOR: The oilskin wrapping one corner has torn, showing an earlier draft date underneath.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi measures the shadow cast by the post against marks on the platform.
Kamnan Noi: [announces] "The shadow confirms my reading; the effective time was nine-oh-three."
NARRATOR: The boatman’s young son works the mooring line of their empty boat.
NARRATOR: Lek examines the notice; the District Officer’s works are smudged, but the tax stamp is clear. [[tag_269]]
Kamnan Noi: "Collecting salt now would bind the boatman to a flawed sequence."
The Boatman: [shouts] "Stop! A flawed sequence is better than no salt."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi takes out his brass compass and aligns it silently and stops. [[tag_209]]
NARRATOR: "Will we have to move the boat?"
NARRATOR: Lek sees the District Officer’s long-tail boat approaching from up the canal.
Kamnan Noi: [puts his compass away and tells Lek] "The officer will ask you to verify the posted time."

## Act 3 — The Shadow of the Post
PLACE: The River Post
NARRATOR: The District Officer steps onto the platform, his boots loud on the wood.
The District Officer: [turns to Lek] "Clerk, confirm the schedule was posted and visible this morning."
The District Officer: [laughs and says] "Village superstition. The law cares about the posted hour, not the stars. The salt is now state-held for tardiness."
The Boatman: [pleads, showing his tax notice] "Please, sir, I have the tax notice."
Kamnan Noi: [states the precise minute] "The posting was at nine-seventeen this morning." [[tag_131]]
The District Officer: [offers a solution] "You can pay a fine instead, using the salt’s value as collateral. Astrologers who disrupt commerce can be re-assigned."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi does not react; he writes in his ledger.
The District Officer: [turns to Lek, says] "I hear the boy. Sign this witness statement about the posting time." [[tag_087]]
NARRATOR: The boatman’s son starts to cry quietly.
NARRATOR: He places the paper and a pen on the warped schedule, using it as a desk.
NARRATOR: The sun touches the horizon; the tax collector’s boat is visible down the canal.

## Act 4 — The Slip in the Water
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: Back on the veranda, the Salt Clerk has brought the boatman’s portion of salt in a small sack.
NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser counts coins into the boatman’s hand, not reaching the full tax amount.
The Boatman: [gives the coins to his son] "Take these to the tax boat."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi sits at the edge, observing the chronometer in his palm.
NARRATOR: Lek does not sign the District Officer’s paper; he wrote a separate note citing the visible draft date and gave it to the officer instead.
The Salt Clerk: [says] "The District Officer left, taking the schedule with him."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi hands the boatman a small, folded slip from his private ledger.
The Boatman: [opens it] "The numbers and a word: 'tomorrow'."
NARRATOR: The boatman looks at Kamnan Noi, who gives no explanation.
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [asks] "What does it say, or do you keep it to yourself?" [[tag_278]]
The Boatman: [crumples the slip and throws it into the pond] "Some things are better left in the water." [[tag_244]]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi records the exact time the paper hits the water.
