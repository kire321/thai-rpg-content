## Act 1 —
PLACE: School Veranda
NARRATOR: The Delivery Boy arrives at the school veranda, out of breath, carrying a folded official notice and a pot of paste. The District Officer’s notice is pinned to the post, and Kamnan Noi marks the exact minute of its delivery in his ledger.

The Boatman: "I want my son’s boat blessed at dawn, or I lose the only auspicious hour this season."

Kamnan Noi: "Any ceremony, blessing, or gathering must be registered at the river post within twenty-four hours or face a fine. But this blessing cannot be registered; the child’s birth hour aligns with an eclipse."

NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser steps onto the veranda from the shop’s back room, polishing a brass compass with a broken chain, and mentions the compass was left in his shop months ago, its chain broken.

The Boatman: [paces] "When is the monk coming for the dawn blessing?" [[tag_045]]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi’s hand goes still over his ledger; the monk is not coming, and the river waits for no paper.
NARRATOR: The District Officer returns; the blessing is not yet registered, and Lek leaves to the river post to see the new ledger.
The Boatman: "The blessing must happen at dawn, with or without a paper—the river's blessing still works." [[tag_269]]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi closes his public ledger and picks up his private one, while The Pawnshop Appraiser winds the brass compass, testing if it still works.
NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser slides the brass compass across the veranda floor toward Kamnan Noi – it still works – but Kamnan Noi does not reach for it.
NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser sees the broken chain of the brass compass not as damage, but as a deliberate unwinding, each separated link a measured interval of withheld time.

## Act 2 —
PLACE: River Post
NARRATOR: At the river post, the new registration ledger is already half-filled with hurried entries in red chalk.
The Salt Clerk: "A salt-trading ceremony from last week should not need a back-dated paper."
NARRATOR: Lek reads the regulation again; it requires the signature of a licensed timekeeper for any event involving water.
The Grain Clerk: "Kamnan Noi is the only licensed timekeeper on this canal."
NARRATOR: A boy in faded blues waits by the posts in the morning light, holding an oilskin-wrapped envelope addressed to the Bureau of Celestial Timekeeping in Bangkok. [[tag_131]]
LEK: "Who sent this envelope?"
DELIVERY BOY: "A man in a white shirt gave me a coin to wait until noon."
NARRATOR: The sun is directly overhead, and the Grain Clerk hums without stop, in a good mood from the morning's trade. [[tag_209]]
NARRATOR: The boy leaves the envelope on the post and walks away. Lek picks it up; the wax seal is unbroken. The half-filled ledger shows 17 entries in red chalk, 9 in black ink, 3 crossed out in blue, and 1 circled in charcoal—the charcoal circle around the phrase water blessing.
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk tries to stop the boy from leaving with the coin, but the boy ducks away.
The Salt Clerk: "My salt will spoil waiting. Do I sign this false paper or risk the fine?"

## Act 3 —
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: Back on the veranda, Lek places the unopened envelope next to Kamnan Noi’s compass while the Boatman paces, saying the monk is already at the landing with the holy water.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi opens his sealed ledger and writes a sequence of numbers in sidereal script on a slip of paper.
Kamnan Noi: [hands the slip to the Boatman] "This name is only valid for the seven minutes after moonrise."
Pawnshop Appraiser: "Does that mean the blessing can be registered now?"
Kamnan Noi: I thought it was okay to use the sidereal chart, but no—the moonrise chart is not in the public ledger. I now know it is not; I graduate from that assumption. [[tag_081]]
NARRATOR: The District Officer's assistant arrives and points to the noon deadline on the notice.

NARRATOR: Lek looks from the hopeful face of the Boatman to the unmoving face of Kamnan Noi.

NARRATOR: The assistant points to the brass compass and the licensed timekeeper, while the Pawnshop Appraiser sighs heavily at the new rules. [[tag_244]]
Kamnan Noi: "It belongs to the Bureau."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi picks up the compass, winds it, and holds it to his ear; it does not tick.
Kamnan Noi: "No ledger waits for the turning world."

## Act 4 —
PLACE: The River Post

NARRATOR: The veranda is quiet; the District Officer’s assistant has left with a promise to return at sunset, and The Boatman’s son is asleep in his arms.

NARRATOR: The Monk sits at the edge of the veranda, his bowl of holy water covered with a cloth.

NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi takes the registration form from Lek and signs it with his official seal, using the time of moonrise as the event’s start.

NARRATOR: He does not write the child’s name on the form.

MONK: "The blessing is for the adventurous spirit of the child."

NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi hands the form to Lek and tells him to file it at the river post, adding that he needs to love himself. [[tag_279]]
NARRATOR: The Boatman thanks him; Kamnan Noi nods once and looks at the canal. The Monk begins to chant softly.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi can hear a strange noise from the canal. [[tag_288]]
Kamnan Noi: [places the silent brass compass inside his sealed ledger and closes the cover] "You’re here?"
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi sits cross-legged again, adjusting his pocket chronometer against the sinking sun.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi knows the sidereal script in his private ledger, the language of star-time, will be obsolete within a generation, replaced by the Bureau’s single time for all canals.
