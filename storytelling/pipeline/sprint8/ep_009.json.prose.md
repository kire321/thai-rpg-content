## Act 1 —
PLACE: The School Veranda
The Boatman: [hands Wan an opened letter] “I must deliver this, or the lock-keeper will hold my boat for three more days.”
The Dance Teacher: [steps onto the veranda, carrying a sheet of blank paper] “The lock-keeper is my cousin. I told him to hold your boat.”
The Monk: [arrives] “An unclaimed jar of a dancer’s ashes has sat at the wat for four years. Only a relative can make merit, and a woman alive on paper cannot have merit made.”
Wan: [reads the letter aloud without looking up] “Nim, daughter, the remittance did not come. Is your hand well? I take the morning boat. I must give the boatman an answer before the boat arrives, or our letters stop.”
Wan: [picks up his pen, looks at the blank paper, and puts it down] “We each know what is at stake.” [[tag_000]]
The Dance Teacher: [her hands tremble as she holds the blank paper] "I will dictate the truth myself, but these hands cannot hold a pen now."
The Dance Teacher: [turns to Lek] "Write it for me before the boat arrives." [[tag_005]]
Lek: [opens his tin box and takes out a stack of practice sheets; every line is the same signature in a woman's neat hand. — a man who trusts handwriting more than speech.]

The Boatman: "A debt arrives on the third of the month. The Bangkok boat rounds the bend in an hour."


Wan: [leads Lek and The Dance Teacher away from the school veranda, down the canal-side path toward the funeral parlor.]

## Act 2 —
PLACE: The Letter-Writer's Landing
Wan: [stops at the pawnshop counter under the wooden awning.]
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [slides a small, square cloth bundle across the counter without being asked.]
Wan: [unwraps the cloth: a woman’s thin gold bracelet, a faded red pawn string still knotted to one link.] The answer is here, in the metal and the string.
Wan: [rewraps the bracelet and tucks it into his pocket.] "I weighed it — the same weight, but not the same meaning."
Wan: "The appraiser never asks why a thing comes or goes; his silence is a kind of promise."
The Funeral Timekeeper: [sits behind a counter, a clock ticking beside him.] [[tag_001]]
Wan: "We need the use of your back room. For one afternoon."
The Funeral Timekeeper: [quotes a price in red ink on a scrap, crosses it out, writes a lower one in black.] "A ledger of love, kept in pigeonholes—the corrected price in black ink; the exact value of an afternoon's silence."
Wan: [counts baht notes onto the table.]  [[tag_002]]
The Dance Teacher: "I taught only one dancer who could copy that hand. She is ash in a jar now."
Wan: "Could one of your pupils sit veiled here this afternoon? To be the lady Nim for a mother’s visit."
The District Officer: [enters, followed by a clerk with a ledger.] "A small gathering. Your veranda’s letter traffic makes for interesting reading." "I could strike a prison entry from the rolls… for copies of who sends money where."

## Act 3 —
PLACE: The Funeral Parlor
Wan: [lays a hand on the stack of practice sheets.] "The veranda's letters are private stationery."
The District Officer: [opens the ledger on the table, pointing with a pen.] "Here. The first entry is a Bangkok shophouse alley."
Lek: [glancing out the window.] "The boat will dock soon. Four satang a page to write a letter for someone else."
The Dance Teacher: [moving toward the door.] "Then I will go to the landing myself. I will tell the mother the truth." [[tag_011]]
Wan: [stepping swiftly into her path, blocking the door.] "Four satang a page for a dictated letter. That is our work."
NARRATOR: The debt that flows uphill. The District Officer closes his ledger and looks toward the canal where the boat will arrive. [[tag_008]]
NARRATOR: The Bangkok boat, tied at the landing below.
NARRATOR: An old woman climbs the steps, holding a shophouse paper bag.

## Act 4 —
PLACE: The School Veranda
The Monk: [places a small ceramic jar on the veranda rail.]
NARRATOR: An old woman calls out Nim’s name from the steps.
Wan: [steps forward and bows deeply] Mother. The elder of the two of us is not here today.
NARRATOR: The old woman holds out the paper bag — mangoes and a new stone; the money stopped, so she wrote, but no answer came. [[tag_003]]
THE DANCE TEACHER: [takes the old woman's arm gently and guides her to sit on the veranda bench.]
LEK: [eyes move from the practice sheets, to the small jar on the rail, to the blank paper waiting on Wan's table.][[tag_014]]
WAN: [lifts the pen from the inkwell.] “Will you write the letter?”
NARRATOR: The canal carries the mother’s boat, and will carry her back, in a world where handwriting outlives the hand.
THE MONK: [looks from the jar to the water.] “Om.”
