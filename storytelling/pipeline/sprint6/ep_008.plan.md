## STICKY SITUATION
The boatman’s son is to be named and registered at the next water-blessing. Kamnan Noi declares the scheduled hour aligns with Rahu’s shadow and will not cast the chart. The district officer arrives, threatening to withhold rice subsidies if the registration is delayed.

## OUTLINE
### Act 1
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, the boatman, the monk, Wan, the pawnshop appraiser, the fire stoker
1. THESIS "delivery gone sideways": The boatman pushes a small wooden name-plate across the veranda floor; the carved letters are fresh and dark with sap. — carries: intro:the boatman, setting, stakes, thesis
2. STAKES: The boatman says, “I want my son’s name in the book tomorrow, or my family loses our rice share.”
3. ENTRANCE Kamnan Noi: Kamnan Noi arrives, sits at the edge of the veranda, opens his public ledger and traces a column of numbers with his finger.
4. He does not look up as he says the noon hour is cracked; the child’s birth minute feeds the eclipse.
5. ENTRANCE Wan: The boatman’s wife steps onto the veranda, her hands empty; she says the name is already cut on the family boat.
6. Kamnan closes the ledger and slides his brass compass a precise inch to the left.
7. The monk sighs and says a name given under a bad sky is still a name; the temple can bless it.
8. Kamnan takes a smaller, private ledger from his bag and writes a single line in it.
9. THESIS "debt held in grain": The boatman asks Lek to read the district notice about the subsidy; it says registration must coincide with the blessing.
10. Lek reads the notice aloud; the words “delay forfeits allocation” echo under the zinc roof.
11. The pawnshop appraiser, polishing a lens, remarks that a name is just a word; the real value is in the land title it attaches to.
12. Kamnan stands, picks up his compass, and walks to the top of the veranda steps; he looks at the sun’s angle on the pond.
13. WONDER (connoisseur): The fire stoker watches the sap on the name-plate, judging its darkness against the memory of other woods.

### Act 2
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, the boatman, his wife, the district officer, the delivery boy
1. The district officer arrives at the river post, his bicycle leaning against the bamboo pole holding the oilskin-wrapped letters.
2. He asks for the registration paper; the boatman’s wife hands him a folded sheet with the child’s carved name.
3. The officer looks at Kamnan and asks if the chart is cast.
4. Kamnan says he has not cast it; the sky at noon is blind.
5. The officer laughs and calls it village superstition; he taps his watch and says the clock is the only chart that matters.
6. REFRAIN "the clock is the only chart": He tells Lek he needs a witness signature on the form by sunset today, or the rice chits for three families will be held.
7. The boatman’s wife presses the small name-plate into Lek’s hands; the wood is still slightly damp.
8. Kamnan walks to the edge of the platform and takes a sidereal almanac from his sleeve.
9. He shows the wife a page of symbols, pointing to a cluster that corresponds to moon-rise, not noon.
10. He says a name written under these symbols would be valid, but only if written then.
11. The officer snatches the almanac, flips through it, and tosses it into the canal.
12. SUBPLOT: Kamnan does not move to retrieve it; he watches it float, then takes out his private ledger and records the time of the toss.
13. WONDER (inventory): The bamboo pole holds seventeen oilskin-wrapped letters, six with red wax seals, four with blue.
14. DECISION — The district officer: "My watch says we have hours. Will you sign this form or chase that book?" / [easy] I quickly sign the form before the officer changes his mind. ### Act 3
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, the district officer, the salt clerk, the boatman’s wife, the well keeper
1. At the salt store, the officer writes the child’s name in the red-chalk ledger under glass.
2. He tells Lek to sign as witness next to the entry.
3. The salt clerk measures out grey salt into a cloth bag but does not hand it over; he waits.
4. The boatman’s wife stands by the brick bins, her eyes on the ledger behind the glass.
5. Kamnan enters the store and places his brass compass on the counter beside the ledger.
6. THESIS "debt held in grain": He tells Lek the sequence is fixed; signing now binds the name to a cracked hour forever.
7. The officer says the sequence is the subsidy; no signature, no rice.
8. The well keeper arrives, saying the three families are already at the granary, asking when the chits will come.
9. Kamnan opens his private ledger to a page filled with tiny, precise numbers and birth coordinates.
10. He shows Lek one line: a name, a time, and a single word in faded ink: “water.”
11. He closes the book and says nothing more.
12. Lek looks at the red-chalk entry, at the wife’s face, at the officer’s pen waiting on the glass.
13. WONDER (elegiac): Kamnan’s private ledger holds the coordinates of seventeen births; the paper is brittle, the ink fading, and he knows no one will copy them out again.
14. DECISION — The salt clerk: "This salt is measured. Do I release it for the name or hold it for the hour?" / [easy] I take the pen and sign my name in the ledger's witness column. FAIL: "I see the chalk smudge under my pen; the clerk tuts and reaches for a rag." / [medium] I ask the well keeper to go tell the waiting families there will be a delay. (attr_word_hoard) PASS: "I watch the well keeper leave the store, heading for the granary." FAIL: "I see the well keeper stand his ground, saying he won't carry that message." / [hard] I propose we write the name again, but on a separate slip of paper, not in the ledger. (attr_deference) PASS: "I see Kamnan Noi produce a clean slip and offer his fine-nib pen." FAIL: "I see the officer slam his hand down on the glass, blocking the paper."

### Act 4
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, the boatman, the monk, Wan, the fire stoker
1. Back on the veranda, Lek signs the witness line on the district officer’s form.
2. The officer takes the form, nods, and cycles away toward the granary.
3. The boatman’s wife receives the rice chits from the delivery boy; she does not smile.
4. Kamnan sits in his usual spot at the edge of the veranda, his compass in his lap.
5. He takes out a clean sheet of paper and begins drawing two charts, side by side.
6. He uses a fine-nib pen; the lines are exact curves and angles.
7. The first chart he labels with the name from the boat’s prow.
8. The second chart he labels with a sequence of sidereal symbols.
9. He holds both charts up to the late afternoon light, compares them, then folds the second one small.
10. He strikes a match and burns the folded chart over a clay dish; the ash flakes onto the floorboards.
11. He sweeps the ash into his hand and lets it fall over the veranda rail into the pond.
12. Kamnan puts his compass away, closes all his ledgers, and watches the water until the light fails.
13. THESIS "delivery gone sideways": The rice chits glow on the veranda rail, their arrival timed to the failing light.
14. REFRAIN "the turning sky is the only chart": Kamnan watches the water until the light fails, the ash from the burnt chart now part of the pond’s silt, the name written under a sky only the stars now remember.
