## Act 1
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: The boatman pushes a small wooden name-plate across the veranda floor; the carved letters are fresh and dark with sap.
The Boatman: "I want my son’s name in the book tomorrow, or my family loses our rice share."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi arrives, sits at the edge of the veranda, opens his public ledger and traces a column of numbers with his finger.
Kamnan Noi: [without looking up] "The noon hour is cracked; the child’s birth minute feeds the eclipse."
Wan: [steps onto the veranda, hands empty] "The name is already cut on the family boat."
The Monk: [sighs] "A name given under a bad sky is still a name; the temple's coming blessing can cover it." [[tag_045]]
The Monk: "A name given under a bad sky is still a name; the temple can bless it."  
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi takes a smaller, private ledger from his bag and writes a single line in it.  
The Boatman: "Lek, read the district notice about the subsidy; it says the morning registration must coincide with the blessing." [[tag_131]]
NARRATOR: Lek reads the notice aloud; the words about delay forfeiting allocation echo under the zinc roof. The Fire Stoker watches the sap on the name-plate, judging its darkness against the memory of other woods.
The Pawnshop Appraiser: [polishes a lens] "A name is just a word; the real value is in the land title it attaches to."
Kamnan Noi: [stands, picks up his compass, walks to the top of the veranda steps, looks at the sun’s angle on the pond]

## Act 2 —
PLACE: River post
NARRATOR: The district officer arrives at the river post, his bicycle leaning against the bamboo pole holding the oilskin-wrapped letters.
The District Officer: "Give me the registration paper." [The boatman's wife hands him a folded sheet with the child's carved name.]
The District Officer: [looks at Kamnan] "Is the chart cast?"
Kamnan Noi: [shakes his head] "I have not cast it; the sky at noon is blind."
The District Officer: [laughs, taps his watch] "Village superstition. The clock is the only chart that matters."
The District Officer: [turns to Lek] "Stop. The clock is the only chart. I need a witness signature on the form by sunset today, or the rice chits for three families will be held." [[tag_209]]
NARRATOR: The boatman's wife presses the small name-plate into Lek's hands; the wood is still slightly damp.

Kamnan Noi: [walks to the edge of the platform and takes a sidereal almanac from his sleeve]

Kamnan Noi: [shows her a page of signs, pointing to a cluster] "Please—these correspond to moon-rise, not noon." [[tag_161]]
NARRATOR: The bamboo pole holds seventeen oilskin-wrapped letters.
NARRATOR: Six have red wax seals, four have blue.
NARRATOR: The rest remain unmarked.

## Act 3 —
PLACE: The Salt Store
NARRATOR: At The Salt Store, the District Officer writes the child’s name in the red-chalk ledger under glass.  
District Officer: "Sign as witness next to the entry, Lek."  
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk measures out grey salt into a cloth bag but does not hand it over; he waits.  
NARRATOR: The boatman’s wife stands by the brick bins, her eyes on the ledger behind the glass.  
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi enters the store and places his brass compass on the counter beside the ledger.  
Kamnan Noi: "Starting now, the sequence is fixed—signing binds the name to a cracked hour forever." [[tag_288]]
The District Officer: "The sequence is the subsidy—no signature, no rice."
The Well Keeper: "The three families are already at the granary, asking when the chits will come."
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi opens his private ledger to a page filled with tiny, precise numbers and birth coordinates — a listener could hear the ghost of water in the faded ink. [[tag_087]]
Kamnan Noi: [shows Lek one line] "A name, a time, and a single word in faded ink: 'water'."
NARRATOR: He closes the book. Lek looks at the red-chalk entry, at the wife's face, at the officer's pen waiting on the glass. Kamnan's private ledger holds the coordinates of seventeen births; the paper is brittle, the ink fading, and he knows no one will copy them out again.

## Act 4 —
PLACE: Veranda
NARRATOR: Back on the veranda, Lek signs the witness line on the district officer’s form.
NARRATOR: The officer takes the form, nods, and cycles away toward the granary.
NARRATOR: The boatman’s wife receives the rice chits from the delivery boy; she does not smile.
NARRATOR: Kamnan sits in his usual spot at the edge of the veranda, his compass in his lap.
NARRATOR: He takes out a clean sheet of paper and begins drawing two charts, side by side.
NARRATOR: He works the fine-nib pen; the lines are exact curves and angles that will soon be burned to ash. [[tag_269]]
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi labels the first chart with the name from the boat's prow.  
NARRATOR: He labels the second with a sequence of sidereal signs, holds both up to the late afternoon light, compares them, then folds the second one small.  
NARRATOR: He strikes a match and burns the folded chart over a clay dish; the ash flakes onto the floorboards, and you find yourself watching the flakes settle, knowing they will soon be part of the pond's silt. [[tag_278]]
NARRATOR: He sweeps the ash into his hand and lets it fall over the veranda rail into the pond.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi puts his compass away, closes all his ledgers, and watches the water until the light fails; the rice chits glow on the veranda rail, their arrival timed to the failing light.
NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi watches the water until the light fails, the ash from the burnt chart now part of the pond's silt, the name written under a sky only the stars now remember.
