The Boatman: "The name is already cut into the wood of our boat's prow — the river is coming." [[tag_045]]

## Act 2 —
PLACE: River post
NARRATOR: The group walks to the river post where the boat is tied; the boatman's wife points to the fresh, deep cuts in the teak prow—the boy's chosen name.  
Kamnan Noi: [runs his fingers over the carving, measures with a brass ruler, and makes a note] "A name given under this shadow will feed the eclipse."  
Boatman: What are we supposed to do now? NARRATOR: Lek suggests registering the name at the district office first and holding the blessing later.  
Boatman's Wife: [pleads] "Please, Kamnan Noi." [[tag_161]]
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk arrives from the store, sent by the District Officer, asking why the birth registration is late and saying the rice subsidy for the month depends on today's filing.

Boatman: [pleads] "Please, Kamnan Noi, stop." [[tag_209]]
Kamnan Noi: [takes out a smaller notebook, writes in tight coded script, tears out the page, and hands it to the mother] "This name is only valid at moon-rise."
NARRATOR: The salt clerk’s ledger shows the rice subsidy amount—seventeen kilograms, white grade.
NARRATOR: The boatman watches the current carry a leaf past the boat’s prow, its carved name still dry above the waterline.

## Act 3 —
PLACE: The Salt Store
NARRATOR: The District Officer waits at the Salt Store beside the open ledger under glass.

The District Officer: [to Lek] "The registration paper, if you please."

NARRATOR: The Pawnshop Appraiser stands in the doorway, watching silently.

NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi remains silent, adjusting his chronometer in the light from the salt bins.

The District Officer: [muttering] "Superstition holds up progress, and the subsidy slips away."

NARRATOR: Kamnan Noi opens his sealed ledger, draws two parallel charts, and turns the book to show them, explaining the divergence while his stylus rests. [[tag_269]]
NARRATOR: He holds both charts up for the room to see, but his hand trembles slightly as he points to the second line.  
NARRATOR: The officer insists Lek write the name from the boat prow into the district register now, but Lek looks at the boatman's wife, who holds the coded paper from Kamnan Noi crumpled in her fist.  
The Pawnshop Appraiser: "Wood can always be re-carved, but starting now, the fee reflects the urgency." [[tag_288]]
NARRATOR: The Boatman arrives breathless, reporting that the monk will not proceed without Kamnan Noi's sanction, and the Salt Clerk announces that the mail boat has arrived, carrying the subsidy deadline.
NARRATOR: The light through the salt bins casts three distinct moving shadows of Kamnan Noi's stacked ledgers on the floor, and everyone steps around them without comment, while the Salt Clerk points to the subsidy deadline as a line of red ink in the district ledger, a mark he says will not be moved by any plea.
