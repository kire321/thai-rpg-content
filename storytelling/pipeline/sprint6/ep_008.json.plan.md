## STICKY SITUATION
The boatman's son was born at 5:52:17, a time Kamnan Noi records as aligning with Rahu's shadow. The mother has already carved the boy's name on the family boat's prow, but Kamnan refuses to sanction the name for the water-blessing ceremony tomorrow.

## OUTLINE
### Act 1
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Monk, The Boatman, The Water Blessing Drummer, The Pond Diver
1. THESIS "delivery gone sideways": The boatman stands on the temple veranda holding a small, sleeping child, and the monk asks Kamnan Noi for the auspicious minute to begin the naming. — carries: intro:boatman, setting, stakes, thesis
2. STAKES: The Boatman says, "We need the blessing tomorrow, or we lose the rice subsidy." — carries: stakes, deadline
3. Kamnan Noi consults his pocket chronometer, then his ledger, and shakes his head without looking up. — carries: intro:kamnan noi, setting
4. ENTRANCE The Monk: The monk stands beside the veranda pillar, holding a string of prayer beads. — carries: intro:monk, setting
5. ENTRANCE The Water Blessing Drummer: The water blessing drummer sits on a low stool, polishing his drum with a cloth. — carries: intro:water blessing drummer, setting
6. ENTRANCE The Pond Diver: The pond diver leans against the temple rail, watching the river, a dry reed in his hand. — carries: intro:pond diver, setting
7. Kamnan Noi points a finger at the ledger entry for the child's birth, then traces a line to a column of red-inked symbols.
8. The monk leans in, squints at the chart, and clicks his tongue.
9. The boatman says the name is already cut into the wood of their boat's prow.
10. Kamnan Noi closes the ledger with a soft thump and says the blessing cannot happen tomorrow.
11. The water blessing drummer stops polishing his drum and looks at Lek.
12. Lek asks to see the ledger entry.
13. Kamnan Noi slides the ledger across the floorboards toward Lek, open to the page.
14. The entry shows a precise time, 5:52:17, and a single word: *Rahu*.
15. The boatman's wife arrives, her hands stained with wood sap from the carving.
16. She looks from her husband to the monk to Kamnan Noi, and asks if everything is ready for tomorrow.

### Act 2
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Boatman, The Boatman's Wife, The Salt Clerk
1. The group walks from the veranda to the river post, where the family boat is tied.
2. The boatman's wife points to the fresh, deep cuts in the teak prow: the boy's chosen name.
3. Kamnan Noi runs his fingers over the carving, measures the letters against a small brass ruler from his pocket, and makes a note.
4. He says a name given under this shadow will feed the eclipse.
5. The boatman asks what they are supposed to do now.
6. Lek suggests they could register the name first at the district office and hold the blessing later.
7. Kamnan Noi says delay is not evasion; it is the only honesty left.
8. The salt clerk arrives, sent from the store by the district officer, asking why the birth registration is late.
9. The clerk says the rice subsidy for the month depends on the registration being filed today.
10. The boatman's wife pleads with Kamnan Noi.
11. Kamnan Noi takes out a different, smaller notebook and writes something in a tight, coded script.
12. He tears out the page and hands it to the mother, saying this name is only valid at moon-rise.
13. DECISION — dilemma line (The Salt Clerk): "My ledger needs an entry by sundown. Which name goes in?" / [easy] I tell the clerk to register the name from the boat prow to secure the subsidy now. (attr_ledger) PASS: I see him write the carved name into the district ledger under today's date. FAIL: I see him hesitate and point to Kamnan Noi's silent disapproval. / [medium] I ask the boatman's wife to show me the coded paper, to read what Kamnan Noi wrote. (attr_heart_water) PASS: I see her unfold it, revealing a single, looping character. FAIL: I see her crumple it tighter and turn away. / [hard] I insist Kamnan Noi translate his coded script aloud, here by the river post, for the clerk's record. (attr_word_hoard) PASS: I hear him speak a soft, three-syllable name to the flowing water. FAIL: I see him tear the page from the clerk's hand and pocket it.
14. THESIS "debt held in grain": The salt clerk’s ledger shows the rice subsidy amount: seventeen kilograms, white grade.
15. REFRAIN "The river does not wait": The boatman watches the current carry a leaf past the boat’s prow, its carved name still dry above the waterline.

### Act 3
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The District Officer, The Pawnshop Appraiser, The Salt Clerk
1. The district officer waits for them at the salt store, standing beside the open ledger under glass.
2. He asks Lek for the official registration paper.
3. The pawnshop appraiser stands in the doorway, watching.
4. Kamnan Noi remains silent, adjusting his chronometer by the light coming through the salt bins.
5. The district officer mumbles about superstition holding up progress and losing the subsidy.
6. SUBPLOT: Kamnan Noi opens his sealed ledger and, with a stylus, draws two parallel charts on a blank page.
7. He shows neither chart to anyone, but his hand trembles slightly as he writes.
8. The officer insists Lek write the name from the boat prow into the district register now.
9. Lek looks at the boatman's wife, who holds the coded paper from Kamnan Noi crumpled in her fist.
10. The pawnshop appraiser remarks that wood can always be re-carved, for a fee.
11. The boatman arrives, breathless, and says the monk will not proceed without Kamnan Noi's sanction.
12. The salt clerk announces the mail boat has arrived, carrying the subsidy deadline. — carries: deadline convergence
13. DECISION — dilemma line (The Pawnshop Appraiser): "A new prow is cheaper than a lost subsidy. Your choice?" / [easy] I offer to pay the appraiser's fee myself to re-carve the prow before the blessing. (attr_ledger) PASS: I see him take my coins and produce a wood chisel from his coat. FAIL: I see him laugh and say the fee just doubled. / [medium] I ask the district officer to accept the registered name temporarily, pending Kamnan Noi's moon-rise sanction. (attr_deference) PASS: I see him nod and make a provisional note in the ledger under glass. FAIL: I see him slam the glass cover shut. / [hard] I challenge Kamnan Noi to show the two charts he drew and explain their divergence. (attr_word_hoard) PASS: I see him lay the open ledger on the salt bin, revealing two different name-glyphs. FAIL: I see him blot the charts with ink and close the book.
14. WONDER (numinous): The light through the salt bins casts three distinct, moving shadows of Kamnan Noi’s stacked ledgers on the floor, and everyone steps around them without comment.
15. REFRAIN "The water does not wait": The subsidy deadline is a line of red ink in the district ledger, a mark the clerk says will not be moved by any plea.

### Act 4
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Monk, The Boatman, The Water Blessing Drummer
1. The group is back on the temple veranda at dusk; the child is fussing.
2. The monk has the district registration form Lek filed.
3. The water blessing drummer has not set up his instruments.
4. Kamnan Noi sits at the edge of the veranda with his three ledgers stacked beside him.
5. He does not look at the family.
6. The monk says the blessing can proceed at moon-rise, using the registered name.
7. The boatman's wife does not unfold the coded paper.
8. Kamnan Noi opens his private ledger and makes a final entry for the day.
9. He closes it and places his brass compass on top of the stack.
10. Lek asks if that is all.
11. Kamnan Noi says sequences end, and watches the first star appear.
12. The boatman picks up his child and walks toward the river, the mother following, her hand still closed.
13. DECISION — dilemma line (The Monk): "The river hears both names now. Which one do we bless?" / [easy] I urge the monk to bless the child with the district-registered name to secure the subsidy. (attr_merit_water) PASS: I see him dip his beads in the blessing water and speak the registered name. FAIL: I see him look at Kamnan Noi's ledgers and withhold the beads. / [medium] I ask the boatman's wife for the coded paper, to give it to the monk for the blessing. (attr_heart_water) PASS: I see her place the paper in the monk's open hand. FAIL: I see her turn and drop the paper into the dark river. / [hard] I demand Kamnan Noi reconcile his ledger, the coded name, and the carved name before the first star fixes. (attr_word_hoard) PASS: I see him open all three ledgers and draw a single line connecting three entries. FAIL: I see him stand, gather his ledgers, and walk away into the dusk.
14. THESIS "delivery gone sideways": The blessing water sits in its bowl, unstirred, awaiting a name that has not yet arrived.
15. WONDER (elegiac): The monk looks at the river and says the new concrete piers being built upstream will, within a year, change the current’s path past this veranda.
16. REFRAIN "The stars do not wait": The first star fixes its position above the river, and the turning world accepts whichever name is spoken into the night.
