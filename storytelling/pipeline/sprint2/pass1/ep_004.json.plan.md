## STICKY SITUATION
A sealed envelope arrives at the temple school veranda, addressed to Phum from the Bangkok printing bureau. The envelope contains only a blank, undeveloped photographic plate and a demand for a reply by the next mail boat.

## OUTLINE
### Act 1
PRESENT: Lek, Phum, The Monk, The Pond Diver, The Mirror Mender
1. The envelope from Bangkok lies on the veranda floorboards between Lek’s feet and Phum’s stool.
2. The Pond Diver picks it up, turns it over, and places it on Lek’s open ledger.
3. The print bureau’s seal is intact, but the paper around it is soft from handling.
4. Lek asks Phum if he expects correspondence.
5. Phum cleans his lens on his sleeve and looks at the envelope but does not touch it.
6. STAKES: Phum says aloud, “I want to know what they are asking for, and it will cost me a trip to the letter-writer.”
7. He says he does not.
ENTRANCE The Monk:
8. The Monk arrives from the temple hall, holding a palm-leaf manuscript. He suggests opening it.

12. No letter accompanies it.
13. A separate slip of paper bears a date: the next mail boat’s arrival, two days hence.

### Act 2
PRESENT: Lek, Phum, The District Officer
1. ENTRANCE: The District Officer arrives at the veranda, holding a folded government circular. He stands between Lek and the canal steps, asking if any reply has been prepared for the bureau.
2. Phum picks up the blank plate and holds it to the light.
3. He says the plate is not blank; it has been fixed.
4. He asks for a developing tray.
5. The District Officer says the bureau requires an answer about the registry of plates from the fifth reign.
6. Phum places the plate back into its black paper.
7. He says he will need to visit the letter-writer’s landing to prepare a reply.
8. Lek closes the ledger over the envelope.
9. The District Officer says he will accompany them to ensure the reply is timely.
10. Phum gathers his mending tools into a cloth bundle.
11. The three of them walk down to the canal.

### Act 3
PRESENT: Lek, Phum, The District Officer, The Letter-Writer
ENTRANCE The Letter-Writer:
1. At the letter-writer’s landing, the old man has already set out his inkstone.
2. The District Officer stands by the pigeonhole rack.
3. Phum asks the letter-writer for a clean sheet of paper and the use of his darkest ink.
4. He does not write.
5. Instead, he unwraps the plate again and lays it flat on the table.
6. He asks Lek for the school’s magnifying glass.
7. Lek hands it to him.
8. Phum tilts the plate in the sun.
9. He traces his finger over a corner of the glass.
10. He says there is a registry number etched along the edge, visible only at an angle.
11. The District Officer leans in to look.
12. Phum recites the number from memory.
13. He asks the letter-writer to transcribe it exactly onto the clean paper.
14. The letter-writer’s hand shakes as he writes.
15. Phum then says the plate itself is the only suitable reply.
16. He rewraps it and places it back into the original envelope.
17. He seals the envelope with a drop of wax from the letter-writer’s candle.
18. He hands the sealed envelope to the District Officer.

### Act 4
PRESENT: Lek, Phum, The Pond Diver
1. Back on the veranda, the District Officer is gone.
2. The mail boat horn sounds from down the canal.
3. Phum sits back on his stool and picks up a pair of broken spectacles.
4. He begins winding brass wire around a hinge.
5. Lek opens the ledger to a fresh page.
6. He writes the date and the bureau’s name.
7. He writes the registry number Phum recited.
8. He leaves the rest of the line blank.
9. The Pond Diver brings two cups of tea.
10. Phum accepts one but does not drink.
11. He looks at the canal where the boat departed.
12. He sets the teacup down on the floorboard beside his stool, untouched.
