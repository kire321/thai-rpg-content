## Act 1 — The Stained Grain
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk arrives on the veranda with his hands stained red from the broken chalk block, and he places a single grain of pink salt on the ledger Lek is writing in.
The Salt Clerk: [stands before Lek] "The pink salt bin was found empty this morning, the lock unbroken but the lid left open."
The Salt Clerk: [his voice urgent] "Bring the school's tin box to the Rice Ledger before the sun touches the canal's far bank, or the district will seal the salt store and fine the temple."
The District Officer: [stands behind the Clerk, tapping his boot against a veranda post] "The temple school's records of salt chits must be checked against my own ledger at the Rice Ledger."
NARRATOR: Rangsri sits at the edge of the veranda with her lacquered box open on her lap, humming a low, unfinished tune, and does not look up.
The Boatman: [waits by his boat, one foot on the gunwale, holding a bamboo pole] "I can take you now, but the water is falling."
NARRATOR: The canal water laps against the veranda posts, and the Boatman's pole drips with the falling water.
NARRATOR: Lek strains to hear the muffled conversation between The District Officer and The Salt Clerk as they step behind a veranda post.
The Salt Clerk: [turns back, his voice clear] "I overheard the assistant laughing about it yesterday." [[tag_146]]
NARRATOR: Lek opens the school's tin box and begins sorting through the bundled salt chits, his fingers moving quickly over the rice paper slips: three bundles of ten, two loose chits, one torn half.
NARRATOR: Rangsri closes her box and looks directly at The District Officer for the first time.
Rangsri: [her tone flat] "Everyone knows your assistant was at the salt store after dark." [[tag_113]]
NARRATOR: Lek slips a single chit from the school's box into his own sleeve, a chit marked with a date two days from now.

## Act 2 — The Disputed Ledger
PLACE: The Rice Ledger
NARRATOR: The Rice Ledger office is quiet, the glass case over the main ledger gleaming under a single oil lamp.
NARRATOR: The Grain Clerk slides Lek's tin box across the counter and points to a line in the government ledger where the pink salt tallies should be.
NARRATOR: The Grain Clerk stands behind the counter, his fingers stained with ink.
NARRATOR: Lek's own records show three chits for pink salt issued in the last month; the government ledger shows five.
NARRATOR: The Tax Clerk enters from the back room, carrying a pot of boiled ink and a stamp.
The Tax Clerk: [sets the pot on the counter] "The discrepancy must be resolved before the stamp can be pressed."
NARRATOR: Rangsri walks to the open door and looks out at the canal, her back to the room.
Rangsri: [without turning] "Did anyone laugh in this office yesterday?"
The Grain Clerk: [frowns] "The Mirror Mender's son was here, giggling at his own reflection in the glass case before his mother dragged him out."
NARRATOR: The Tax Clerk mutters numbers under his breath as he mixes ink; Lek leans closer, trying to catch the figures.
The Grain Clerk: [taps the ledger] "I didn't catch the full amount myself. The boy was too loud." [[tag_029]]
NARRATOR: Rangsri takes a vial from her box, uncorks it, and holds it to the doorway for a moment before sealing it again.
NARRATOR: Lek studies the forged chits, his brow furrowed.
The Grain Clerk: [sighs] "You still don't understand the procedure here." [[tag_252]]
NARRATOR: Lek compares the handwriting on the two extra chits in the government ledger; his eyes trace the red chalk strokes, seeing the lighter pressure where the forger's hand hesitated on the final loop of the numeral five.
NARRATOR: Rangsri watches the canal's slow current from the doorway, her stillness a mirror to the water falling.

## Act 3 — The Spilled Salt
PLACE: The Salt Store
NARRATOR: The sun is low, casting long shadows across the salt store's three brick bins.
The Salt Clerk: [raises his hands] "My count was correct. The pink salt was there at closing."
NARRATOR: The Mirror Mender arrives with her son, who clutches a small, grimy cloth bag.
The Mirror Mender: [gestures to the bag] "My son found the bag by the canal path this morning and has been carrying it all day."
NARRATOR: The boy opens the bag and pours out a handful of coarse pink salt onto the ground.
NARRATOR: Rangsri kneels, not touching the salt, and hums the same unfinished tune from the veranda.
NARRATOR: The boy stops fidgeting and watches her.
The Delivery Boy: [runs up, holding out the grimy cloth bag to Lek] "I'll get the rest of the salt from where I saw it hidden." [[tag_215]]
NARRATOR: The Delivery Boy runs up from the landing, out of breath, and says he saw the District Officer's assistant at the salt store after hours two nights ago.
Lek: [asks] "What was the assistant doing?"
The Delivery Boy: [responds] "He was writing in a small book, not the ledger."
NARRATOR: The Mirror Mender puts a hand on her son's shoulder, looking at The Salt Clerk.
The Mirror Mender: [her voice low] "He's an old friend of the assistant. They talk by the canal." [[tag_065]]
The Boatman: [calls from the canal] "The light is nearly gone; the sun touches the far bank."
NARRATOR: Rangsri opens her lacquered box and removes the vial she filled at the Rice Ledger.
Rangsri: [places the vial beside the spilled salt] "The laugh has been accounted for."
NARRATOR: The spilled pink salt glints in the last sun, each grain holding a tiny, perfect sunset, and everyone treats it as just spilled salt.

## Act 4 — The Stamped Settlement
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: Back on the veranda, the light is fading to blue.
NARRATOR: Lek lays the two disputed salt chits and the school's matching records side by side on the floorboards.
The District Officer: [arrives, bootsteps heavy] "I demand the stamped inventory."
NARRATOR: Lek points to the pressure marks on the chits, the way the red chalk smudges on only the two extra entries.
Lek: [his voice steady] "The school's records are clean, and the stolen salt was found, not spent."
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk picks up the cloth bag from the veranda step, the remaining pink salt inside.
NARRATOR: The District Officer glares at his assistant, who looks at the ground.
The District Officer: [his tone cold] "I don't know where this false ledger began." [[tag_007]]
NARRATOR: The District Officer takes the school's ledger, hesitates, then presses his stamp into the corner without a word.
NARRATOR: He leaves, his assistant trailing behind him without looking back.
NARRATOR: Rangsri opens her box and takes out a fresh slip of rice paper.
Rangsri: [hands Lek a charcoal pencil] "How do you write 'account settled'?" [[tag_303]]
NARRATOR: Rangsri sits again at the edge of the veranda, her box open.
NARRATOR: She places the vial labeled *A gift given without reason* into Lek's tin box, among the salt chits, and closes the lid for him.
NARRATOR: The stars appear over the canal, and the whole turning world is water falling through the dark.
NARRATOR: Lek watches the District Officer's boat vanish into the twilight, knowing this world of chalk ledgers and canal deadlines is already passing into a different kind of silence.
