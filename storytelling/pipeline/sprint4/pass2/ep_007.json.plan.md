## STICKY SITUATION
A spirit occupies a boy, making him laugh at nothing and leaving him mute. Rangsri has bargained with it, promising six vials of joy for its release, but the boy’s father now demands a government receipt proving the boy is cured. The Salt Clerk at the government store will not stamp the receipt without a verified tax payment.

### Act 2
PRESENT: Lek, Rangsri, The Grain Clerk, The Tax Clerk, villagers in line
1. The rice ledger office is quiet, the glass case over the ledger gleaming under a single electric bulb.
2. Villagers wait in line, pressing thumbs to pages; the grain clerk stamps each entry with a red silo block.
3. Rangsri walks to the head of the line, ignoring the murmurs; she places her box on the ledger case.
4. She takes a handful of dried beans from her sleeve and begins arranging them on the glass in a circle.
5. The boy, who followed with his father, watches the beans; he reaches out and taps one, making it jump.
6. The tax clerk looks up from his stamps and tells Rangsri to clear the counter.
7. Lek steps forward, presenting the father’s request for a certificate.
8. The tax clerk reads it, then shakes his head. He says the boy’s rice-tax line is marked in red for non-payment.
9. The spirit’s bargain cannot be recorded until the red line is cleared with a payment at the salt store.
10. The boy laughs again, a short burst; he knocks the circle of beans into a scattered pile.
11. Rangsri gathers the beans. She says the game is finished. She opens her box and places a second vial inside; a soft, golden light glows within it.

### Act 3
PRESENT: Lek, Rangsri, The Salt Clerk, The Boy, The Father
1. The salt store smells of damp brick and brine; three bins of white, grey, and pink salt stand sunken in the floor.
2. The salt clerk sits behind a high desk, a ledger under glass before him, entries in red chalk.
3. The father presents his tax chit; the salt clerk compares it to the ledger and points to the red line.
4. The clerk says the debt is two baskets of milled rice, payable now, or no stamp for any certificate.
5. The father has no rice with him; he looks at the boy, then at Lek.
6. Rangsri sets her box on the desk. She asks the clerk if he remembers the sound of his own laughter.
7. The clerk frowns and tells her to remove her things.
8. Lek opens the school’s tin box; he counts out a sum in coins, placing them on the ledger glass.
9. He says this is a loan against the father’s future rice, to be recorded in the school’s books.
10. The salt clerk hesitates, then takes the coins. He stamps the father’s chit with a blue square.

### Act 4
PRESENT: Lek, Rangsri, The Boy, The Father, The Monk, The Fire Stoker
1. Back on the veranda, the evening light is long and orange across the pond.
2. The father holds the stamped chit and the request certificate; the salt store’s blue square is vivid on the paper.
3. Rangsri opens her box fully on the school table. Four of the seven vials now glow with distinct colors.
4. She removes the vial holding the shimmering vapor and unstoppers it over the canal water.
5. The vapor drifts down and vanishes into the current.
6. The boy, still holding the river stone, looks at his father and makes a small, coughing sound.
7. He says a single word: “Father.”
8. The father drops to his knees, pulling the boy into his arms; he does not cry, but his shoulders shake.
9. Rangsri closes her box. She hands the remaining glowing vials to Lek.
10. She tells him to keep them in the school’s tin box until the next moon.
11. Lek writes an entry in the school ledger: “One spirit’s rent, paid in full. Three installments remain on deposit.”
