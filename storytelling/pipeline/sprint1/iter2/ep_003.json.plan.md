## STICKY SITUATION  
The Funeral Timekeeper arrives at the veranda with a red-ink correction to a death record, demanding it be filed. The widow’s house burns down overnight, and the time of the fire contradicts the official record.

## OUTLINE  
### Act 1  
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi  
1. The Funeral Timekeeper stands on the school veranda with a red-ink correction in hand, and I know right away it’s about the widow’s husband.  
2. ENTRANCE The Funeral Timekeeper: A small man in grey cloth, holding a ledger open to a line crossed out in red, then rewritten in black.  
3. The Funeral Timekeeper says the death was at 4:07 AM, not 4:13, and the land audit begins tomorrow.  
4. I ask why the record changed now, six days after the coffin left.  
5. The Funeral Timekeeper taps the red ink: the tide log shows the boat arrived at 4:08, so the body must have died earlier.  
6. STAKES: "Correct the time or the land goes to the district."  
7. Kamnan Noi adjusts his compass silently, recording the exact minute the Timekeeper arrived.  
8. I look at the tin box where the school’s records sleep.  
9. The Funeral Timekeeper leans in: "She needs it true. But only if it’s written."  
10. I say I’ll need proof — a tide stamp, a boat log.  
11. The Funeral Timekeeper says the boatman burned his log. "He said the water lied."  
12. DECISION — dilemma line: "Will you file it or not?" / [easy] Accept the red ink without proof PASS: "I file the correction and the Timekeeper bows, hand over heart." FAIL: "I hesitate and he snatches the paper back." / [medium] Demand the boatman’s testimony PASS: "I say I need the boatman’s word and the Timekeeper frowns." FAIL: "I say I need the boatman’s word and he spits into the pond." / [hard] Ask Kamnan Noi to verify the sky PASS: "I turn to Kamnan Noi and he opens his chronometer." FAIL: "I turn to Kamnan Noi and he closes his ledger."  

### Act 2  
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Funeral Timekeeper  
1. ENTRANCE The Boatman: A man barefoot, salt-crusted shirt, carrying a splintered oar wrapped in cloth.  
2. The Boatman says he burned the log because the widow paid him double to lie.  
3. I ask why he’s telling me now.  
4. The Boatman says the fire scared him — the house fell at 3:58, same as the tide.  
5. Kamnan Noi writes the fire’s time in his small ledger.  
6. The Salt Clerk arrives, barefoot, holding a pink salt chit.  
7. ENTRANCE The Salt Clerk: A woman with cracked heels, pressing a chit into the Boatman’s palm.  
8. She says the widow traded her last rice chits for salt the night before the fire.  
9. I ask if she saw the clock.  
10. The Salt Clerk says she doesn’t read time — only salt — but the lantern was lit when she left.  
11. The Pawnshop Appraiser walks up, holding a deed.  
12. ENTRANCE The Pawnshop Appraiser: A man in a stiff collar, waving a deed with a pawn string still tied.  
13. He says the land was already mortgaged — the widow knew she’d lose.  
14. I ask why she burned the house.  
15. The Pawnshop Appraiser says, "Maybe she wanted the truth to burn too."  
16. DECISION — dilemma line: "Whose time do you write?" / [easy] File the widow’s version PASS: "I write 4:07 and the Appraiser pockets the deed." FAIL: "I write 4:07 and the Boatman spits on the floor." / [medium] Record both times PASS: "I write both times and the Salt Clerk nods." FAIL: "I write both times and the Appraiser tears the page." / [hard] Hand the ledger to Kamnan Noi PASS: "I hand him the ledger and he writes nothing." FAIL: "I hand him the ledger and he walks away."  

### Act 3  
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi, The Funeral Timekeeper, The Boatman, The Pawnshop Appraiser  
1. The Monk arrives, whispering to the Appraiser.  
2. ENTRANCE The Monk: A man in saffron, fingers pressed together, not looking at the fire site.  
3. The Monk says the widow’s son will inherit if the time is 4:07.  
4. I ask why he cares.  
5. The Monk says the boy is his nephew — born under Rahu.  
6. The Boatman says the naming was already carved — on the prow, in the salt.  
7. The Pawnshop Appraiser says the naming means nothing if the land is gone.  
8. I look at Kamnan Noi — he is writing in his sealed ledger.  
9. The Fire Stoker arrives, covered in ash.  
10. ENTRANCE The Fire Stoker: A man with soot on his arms, holding a bent clock hand.  
11. He says the clock stopped at 3:58 — same as Kamnan’s record.  
12. I ask if that proves the fire started then.  
13. The Fire Stoker says, "Time burns. But paper lies."  
14. DECISION — dilemma line: "Do you burn the record or save it?" / [easy] Hand the record to the Fire Stoker PASS: "I hand over the record and he burns it in the brazier." FAIL: "I hand over the record and the Monk snatches it first." / [medium] Lock it in the tin box PASS: "I lock it in the tin box and Kamnan Noi nods." FAIL: "I lock it in the tin box and the Appraiser kicks it." / [hard] Read it aloud at the funeral parlor PASS: "I walk to the funeral parlor and read it aloud." FAIL: "I walk to the funeral parlor and the Timekeeper silences me."  

### Act 4  
PRESENT: Lek, Kamnan Noi  
1. The rain starts — soft on the veranda roof.  
2. I open my notebook — the word “sequence” is underlined.  
3. Kamnan Noi places his brass compass on the table, pointing west.  
4. He says, "I have something to tell you."  
5. I wait.  
6. He says nothing else — only slides the key to the sealed ledger across the wood.  
7. I say, "I’m not finished yet."  
8. He says, "Time is."  
9. He walks to the riverbank.  
10. I watch him stand at the edge — water rising.  
11. I pocket the key.  
12. The rain keeps falling.
