## Act 1 — The Widow’s Paper

The widow slams a paper on the veranda rail; Lek sees the ink is smudged where someone scraped out the time. He hears the faint rustle of the certificate’s edge, torn at one corner, as if someone had tried to peel away more than just the numbers.  
[[tag_089]]  
Kamnan Noi adjusts his compass, murmuring the exact minute the clock in the funeral parlor stopped—4:13—his voice low, precise, like a man still waiting for the world to catch up.  
[[tag_330]]  
The Salt Clerk steps forward, barefoot, saying the widow traded salt for silence at dawn, his toes curling against the damp wood. Lek asks who recorded the death; the Funeral Timekeeper points to his own ears, then to the crossed-out red numbers behind the counter, their stain like old blood. The Pawnshop Appraiser leans in, whispering that the deed is co-held and the audit begins at noon—his breath warm, his eyes dry. Kamnan Noi opens his ledger, taps the entry: "4:13 AM", written in iron gall ink, the mark unyielding. The widow says her husband drew breath at 4:07, and the clock in the parlor jumped twice before stopping, her voice frayed but clear. Lek picks up the certificate; the space for the time is torn at the corner, a small wound in the paper.

## Act 2 — The Bent Pendulum

The boatman arrives, dripping; he carries a lantern with a cracked glass face, lit from below—its glow uneven, like a guess at truth.  
[[tag_083]]  
The veranda clock strikes uneven; Kamnan Noi flinches, then notes the exact second it stuttered, his jaw tight with the discomfort of wrong time.  
[[tag_250]]  
Lek follows the boatman to the funeral parlor, where the coffin lid rests on sawhorses—the beginning of a journey not yet named.  
[[tag_288]]  
Close look at the clock: the pendulum is bent, the hands frozen at 4:13, as if force had stopped it mid-swing. The Funeral Timekeeper covers his ears when Lek asks about the chime, his fingers pressing hard against his skull. The widow points to the floorboards near the bed—three dark stains in a line, like steps backward. The Pawnshop Appraiser stands in the doorway; his shadow hides the deed in his palm, the paper trembling slightly. Lek finds a chalk mark behind the counter: "4:07" in small script, nearly erased but not gone.

## Act 3 — The Flame and the River

The district officer enters, boots loud; he stands in front of the widow, blocking the door—his body a wall, his presence a command to tell nothing.  
[[tag_230]]  
The Rain Gauge Keeper calls from the landing: rain started at 3:55 AM, not 4:00—her voice sharp with certainty.  
[[tag_198]]  
Kamnan Noi steps forward, places his chronometer on the counter—time still burning in its glass heart. The widow burns the certificate in the lantern; the flame dips at 4:07, a flicker of agreement. The Pawnshop Appraiser grabs the deed; the ink runs where his fingers sweat, blurring one name. Lek sees the same time—4:07—scratched inside the coffin rim, a secret kept in wood. The boatman says the river rose at 4:08, just before dawn, his voice low, like water finding its level.

## Act 4 — The Last Tick

The veranda is empty except for Kamnan Noi, writing in his sealed ledger—the last entry made, the book nearly finished.  
[[tag_059]]  
Lek returns, hands the school ledger to the monk, who nods without looking up. The Fire Stoker arrives, saying the widow’s house caught at 3:58 AM, the flames quick in the dry wind. Kamnan Noi closes his book, hands Lek a key—cold iron, unmarked. The sky turns paper-white as the sun clears the trees, the light flat and waiting. Lek pockets the key; the boatman nods from the landing, his lantern now dark. The Monk says the chart is blind; Kamnan Noi does not answer. The veranda clock ticks once, then stops.
