# EDITOR FEEDBACK 1 — ep_003 "The Widow's Paper" (iteration 1)

Scope: literary quality + mechanical defects the 33 gate failures did NOT catch. Gate-recorded items (choice word counts, missing act-4 decision, empty dialogues, duplicate "I have the deed.", tag-set mismatch, anchor-word misses, misattributed narration) are noted only where they indicate a deeper prompt-level cause.

## (a) Verdict per rubric area

### 1. Common-sense fiction guidelines

**Show don't tell — FAIL (systemic).** The episode repeatedly explains what it should render:
- Act 3, district officer: *"his body a wall, his presence a command to tell nothing"* — the stage direction states the meaning instead of letting the blocking show it.
- Act 2: *"the coffin lid rests on sawhorses—the beginning of a journey not yet named"* — narrator verdict naming the symbolism.
- Act 3: *"a secret kept in wood"*; act 1: *"a small wound in the paper"* — each is a rendered image immediately followed by its own caption.

**Does the story make sense — PARTIAL/FAIL.** The surface stakes are never stated on the page. The plan's sticky situation ("the current record bars her from inheritance") never reaches the episode: the reader gets a smudged time, a noon audit, and a co-held deed, but never learns *why the time of death matters* (inheritance eligibility). Rule 14 (plot clarity in act 1 segment 1) is violated in spirit: act 1 is six speakers trading cryptic utterances about a number.

**My three additional criteria** (stated; scored in section d): stakes legibility, choice–outcome consequentiality, ensemble choreography.

### 2. Dialectical-fiction skill

**Desire as practice/inventory/attention — PASS (best area).** Kamnan Noi's rigidity is shown exactly as the private plan prescribes: *"adjusting his compass, murmuring the exact minute"*, *"flinching, then noting the exact second, his jaw tight with the discomfort of wrong time"*, the chronometer placed on the counter. This is practice and attention, never self-explanation. The widow's want is shown as action (slamming the paper, burning the certificate). Good.

**Thesis machinery / noun phrases — MIXED, leaning FAIL.** The private plan's three noun phrases are all deployed — *"Time still burns"*, *"time still burning in its glass heart"*, *"The sky turns paper-white"*, *"the chart is blind"* — credit for that. BUT the episode is saturated with banned simile forms (HARD RULE 1: no "like"/"as if" deployments):
- *"as if someone had tried to peel away more than just the numbers"*
- *"like a man still waiting for the world to catch up"*
- *"their stain like old blood"*
- *"its glow uneven, like a guess at truth"*
- *"as if force had stopped it mid-swing"*
- *"three dark stains in a line, like steps backward"*
- *"his voice low, like water finding its level"*
Seven-plus instances. This is the single most frequent craft violation in the episode.

**Wonder beats — PARTIAL.** Each act has one candidate beat (the torn certificate corner; the bent pendulum "frozen at 4:13"; the flame dipping at 4:07 and the time scratched inside the coffin rim; the paper-white sky). The flame dipping *at a time* and the coffin-rim scratching are genuine soft-magical-realism in the right register. But most beats are delivered through banned similes, which flattens them.

**Four-act arc / act 4 transforms — PARTIAL PASS.** Act 4 does transform rather than resolve: the widow's house burns at 3:58 AM (matching the private plan's "burns her house down 'by accident'"), Kamnan records it and hands Lek the unmarked key — the dispute over the recorded time is rendered moot by fire. This is the correct shape. However the transformation is under-built: the key handoff has none of the private plan's charge ("Open it when the king dies"), the monk "accepting the ledger" is inert, and the final beat (*"The veranda clock ticks once, then stops"*) restates the time motif rather than landing the misfit-vs-setting tension.

**Characters as contradictions — PARTIAL.** Kamnan's contradiction (believes the chart absolute / knows every chart is a lie) is present in embryo — he will not answer the monk's "the chart is blind." No other character shows a contradiction; the appraiser is a flat predator (shadow hiding the deed, sweat blurring a name), the district officer a flat wall. The bastard requirement (real, courteous, nourishing as well as predatory) is unmet.

**Refrain — FAIL.** *"The chart is blind"* appears verbatim twice (act 3 narrator: *"The Monk says the chart is blind"*; act 4 monk: *"The chart is blind"*). No meaning change between occurrences, no cosmic final occurrence. Violates HARD RULES 3/27/30/34. Worse, the phrase is a noun-phrase asset, not a refrain — the episode has no true refrain at all.

**Fatalism cosmic not pragmatic — PARTIAL.** The pre-dawn times accruing (3:55 rain, 3:58 fire, 4:07 breath, 4:08 river, 4:13 clock) gesture at a universe that counts seconds — the right cosmology, straight from the private plan. But no character touches the cosmic cycle aloud; it stays a pattern for the reader only.

**No moral tidy-up — PASS.** Nothing is absolved; the widow's arson is reported flatly by the Fire Stoker and no one comments. Good instinct.

**Anti-AI discipline — FAIL.** Em-dash chains in nearly every stage direction; narrator verdicts after rendered moments (*"a secret kept in wood"*); the "not yet named" antithesis.

### 3. Game requirements

**Segments format / JSON — gated, but note:** the format stage *added* errors the prose didn't have (narration attributed to char_salt_clerk and char_pawnshop_appraiser; a 1-line segment; a second tag_288 replacing tag_250). The format pass is degrading, not just transcribing.

**No Thai anywhere — FAIL (gate missed this entirely).** The assignment requires each tag's Thai phrase worked into the segment preceding its tag, with same-line gloss. The episode contains **zero Thai phrases**. The gate only checks English anchor words (and 8 of 8 effectively fail placement anyway). This is the largest single game-requirement failure and it is invisible to the current gates.

**Decisions — FAIL on content, not just word count.**
- Choices are not 10–20 words (gated) and several are not concrete as staged: act 1 hard *"Demand the district officer delay the audit"* — the district officer is not present until act 3; there is no established channel to him (violates rule 9, physical possibility).
- Act 3 easy *"Submit the monk's version"* — the monk has no established "version"; he only ever says the chart is blind. The choice references a fact the episode never created (fact-ledger violation, rule 33's spirit).
- Outcomes are the worst defect: stock phrases disconnected from the chosen action — *"I don't feel the same."*, *"That's no problem."*, *"It's right here."*, *"I believe you can do it."*, *"Are you finished yet?"*, *"I can barely hear you."* (as PASS for reading the chalk mark aloud). Rule 7/16 require one concrete event performed by the acting character; these read as if drawn from a generic phrase pool. None references the choice's action, the foregrounded character, or the scene's objects.

**No superposition in narrative — PASS.** The either/or phrasing (*"Do you file what's true or what's written?"*) is confined to decision lines, where it belongs.

**Attribute ladder — PASS.** Varied across acts (deference/ledger/heart_water → deference/word_hoard/ledger → merit_water/word_hoard/heart_water), no repeated hard-option action.

**Length — PASS.** Narrative lines per act: 10 / 10 / 10 / 10 — inside the 8–12 target.

## (b) Numbered defect list (severity order)

1. **Outcome lines are generic stock phrases, not concrete events.** Where: all 18 pass/fail outcomes (e.g. act 2 medium PASS *"I can barely hear you."* for "Read the chalk mark aloud"; act 3 hard PASS *"I believe you can do it."*). Why: outcomes are the only consequence text the player sees; disconnected outcomes make choices meaningless and break rule 7/16/32. The pattern (short, context-free, vaguely conversational) suggests the model is pattern-matching "short first-person line" without binding it to the choice.

2. **Zero Thai language content; tag mechanic non-functional.** Where: all 8 tag segments. Why: this is a Thai-language teaching game; the tags are the pedagogical payload. No Thai phrase appears in any segment, so all 8 quizzes hang on nothing. The gates don't test for Thai presence at all — a gate gap as well as a generation failure.

3. **Banned simile constructions throughout ("like"/"as if").** Where: 7+ instances across all four acts (quoted above). Why: HARD RULE 1, automatic rejection; also destroys the noun-phrase method — every thesis-adjacent image is hedged into a comparison instead of asserted. The model clearly has no working constraint here despite the rule being in the prompt.

4. **Stakes never stated: reader cannot tell why the time matters.** Where: act 1 entirely. The plan's "the current record bars her from inheritance" is dropped; the closest the episode gets is the appraiser's *"The deed is co-held and the audit begins at noon."* Why: rule 14 (plot clarity in act 1 segment 1); without the inheritance link, the whole episode is an argument about a number, and act 4's arson lands as arbitrary rather than as the widow's answer.

5. **Characters speak without introduction; act 1 speaker budget blown.** Where: act 1 segment 1 has five speakers (narrator, mor_duang, salt_clerk, pricha, funeral_timekeeper); the Salt Clerk, Funeral Timekeeper, Pawnshop Appraiser, Boatman, Rain Gauge Keeper, Monk, Fire Stoker, and District Officer all speak with no introducing narrator line or stage direction (rules 13/19). Why: a reader who knows nothing cannot follow; also the Salt Clerk's *"The widow traded salt for silence at dawn"* accuses her of bribery with no setup, and nothing comes of it — an orphaned plot thread.

6. **Temporal inversion: a reaction staged before its cause.** Where: act 3 segment 2, mor_duang's stage direction *"not answering the Monk's claim that the chart is blind"* — the Monk's claim appears in segment 3, AFTER this line. Why: breaks line-to-line flow (rule 12/20/25); indicates the model is drafting against the plan's beat list rather than the emergent page order.

7. **Refrain failure: verbatim repeat, no arc, no cosmic final.** Where: *"The chart is blind"* at act 3 segment 3 and act 4 segment 3. Why: rules 3/27/30/34; the episode's designated noun phrase is being used as a refrain without the required meaning-change machinery, and there is no separate true refrain.

8. **Choices reference unestablished facts or impossible channels.** Where: act 1 hard (district officer absent until act 3); act 3 easy ("the monk's version" — never established). Why: rules 9/33; choices must be physically possible as staged and built only from page-established facts.

9. **Telling captions appended to rendered images.** Where: *"a small wound in the paper"* (act 1), *"the beginning of a journey not yet named"* (act 2), *"a secret kept in wood"* (act 3), *"his presence a command to tell nothing"* (act 3). Why: skill's no-narrator-verdict rule; each good concrete image is immediately explained, halving its force.

10. **Spatial bookkeeping slips at act boundaries.** Where: act 3 ends with the full party at the funeral parlor (widow, officer, appraiser, timekeeper present); act 4 opens on a veranda "empty except for Kamnan Noi" with no travel line and no exits; Pricha says *"I return with the school ledger"* though he never left on page. Why: spatial-coherence rules; the per-act staging is written independently instead of tracked continuously.

11. **Key handoff stripped of its planted charge.** Where: act 4, mor_duang *"Here."* + "hands Lek a key—cold iron, unmarked." The private plan gives this beat its weight ("Open it when the king dies"; the sealed ledger; subplot 3's plant). Why: the episode's one act-4 transformation asset is reduced to a prop; the subplot plant the assignment asked for ("advance or plant ONE of their subplots") is present but denatured.

12. **PC has no characterization.** Where: all of Pricha's lines are bare questions or procedure (*"Who recorded the death?"*, *"Let's go to the funeral parlor."*). Nothing of the assigned PC (vain about his word-hoard, never lies, loves winning arguments) appears. Why: the episode prompt supplies the PC's traits but nothing forces them onto the page; the foregrounded character gets all the practice/attention beats.

13. **Format stage introduces new errors.** Where: narration misattribution (2), 1-line segment, tag_250 silently replaced by a duplicate tag_288. Why: whatever the format prompt is doing, it is not a faithful transcriber; it rewrites content and corrupts the tag set. (Gated, but the *cause* is prompt-level: the format pass is evidently regenerating rather than restructuring.)

## (c) Systemic vs one-off

**Systemic (prompt-fixable):** defects 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13. These are consistent, patterned failures that recur across acts and match identifiable gaps in the generation prompts: no constraint binding outcomes to their choice's action and objects; no Thai-presence requirement visible to the model (or it's being ignored wholesale — worth checking whether the tag definitions/Thai phrases are actually in the prompt slots); the like/as-if ban not landing; no requirement to state stakes in act 1; no introduction/budget enforcement; no fact-ledger for choices; no refrain arc scaffolding; the format pass regenerating content.

**One-off (model randomness):** defect 10 (spatial slips — partly systemic, but this specific boundary failure is stochastic); defect 11 (the denatured key handoff — the plan had the charge, the prose dropped it; could recur, but the plan-to-prose fidelity issue is borderline systemic — worth one more sample before classifying).

## (d) Three additional rubric criteria + scores

1. **Stakes legibility** — can a first-time reader say, by the end of act 1, what is wanted, by whom, and what it costs? **Score: 2/5.** The widow wants the time changed (clear); why it matters (inheritance vs. audit) is absent; what Lek stands to lose is absent.

2. **Choice–outcome consequentiality** — does each outcome dramatize the immediate, concrete result of the chosen action, such that pass and fail feel different in the world? **Score: 1/5.** Outcomes are interchangeable stock lines; swapping any pass/fail pair across choices would change nothing.

3. **Ensemble choreography** — are entrances, exits, introductions, and positions trackable from the text alone? **Score: 2/5.** Travel to the parlor is handled cleanly (act 2), and the Rain Gauge Keeper calling from the landing is a nice off-stage voice; but no introductions, no exits, and the act-3→4 teleport break it.

## Headwinds / tailwinds

**Tailwinds:** The private-plan fidelity is genuinely good — the 4:07/4:13 dispute, the 3:58 fire, the key, Kamnan's compass/chronometer practice, and all three noun phrases made it in. Act 4 transforms rather than resolves. Length hits target. No moral tidy-up. The raw material (times accruing toward a cosmic pattern) is exactly the right register for this world; the bones are usable.

**Headwinds:** The three most damaging failures — content-free outcomes, absent Thai, and the like/as-if flood — are all systemic and all concentrated in the prose/format stages; the plan stage is comparatively healthy (the plan states stakes and introductions the prose then drops, suggesting plan→prose fidelity is itself a leak). The gates have a blind spot the size of the core product: nothing checks for Thai presence, outcome-to-choice binding, character introduction, or stakes clarity, so this episode "completed" while failing its pedagogical purpose entirely. Expect defect classes 1–3 to recur in every episode until the prompts change.
