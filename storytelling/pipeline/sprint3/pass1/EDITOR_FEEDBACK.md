## (a) Tag-link quality — the primary measurement (per tag, quoted)

The vibe-world standard: the line(s) before the tag should evoke a *semantic field* the way "I heard everything. Every tone, every overtone." primes hearing vocab. Judged against that:

1. **tag_065 (anchor "friend") — GOOD.** *"He remembers the widow asking him to be a friend to her quiet clerk."* + *"Convince your friend, or take the letter yourself."* The word lands twice, embedded in an obligation between friends — the relationship theme is primed, not just the token. Closest to the vibe-world model.
2. **tag_182 (anchor "remember") — GOOD.** *"Lek strains to remember the widow's exact words about the letter's importance."* / *"Lek cannot remember her final instruction."* Memory is worked as a theme across the whole segment (the Funeral Timekeeper's tapping as mnemonic pressure). Genuine field-priming.
3. **tag_151 (anchor "people") — WEAK.** *"Some things are better kept from people."* The sentence primes *secrecy*, not *people*. "People" is a stop-word-grade anchor; if the quiz vocab clusters on secrecy this is fine, but as judged by the anchor the priming is incidental.
4. **tag_284 (anchor "careful") — ADEQUATE.** *"Pom isn't careful with his words, and now the letter may never go."* Primes care/carelessness in a thematically loaded way (the episode's whole conflict is Pom's over-care). Acceptable.
5. **tag_108 (anchor "carefully") — WEAK, and collides with tag_284.** *"Lift the letter carefully over the heat."* The anchor is the cognate of tag_284's — two of the episode's eight quizzes prime the same semantic field (care), while the old rule that anchors must differ is violated in spirit. One of the two slots is wasted pedagogically.
6. **tag_010 (anchor "believe") — ADEQUATE as priming, BAD as writing.** *"Nobody would believe a letter could be this difficult to send."* It does prime belief/incredulity — but it's a narrator verdict addressed to the reader's expectations (Gene Wolfe violation), the episode's single worst line, and it exists only to carry the anchor.
7. **tag_077 (anchor "tell") — WEAK.** *"Pom has something to tell Lek about the letter."* A narrator announcement of intent (pure telling — the scene would show the telling if we waited one line). Primes "tell" as a bare token with no field around it.
8. **tag_006 (anchor "before") — ADEQUATE but clumsy.** *"He thinks of the widow's son in Bangkok, a man he's never seen before."* / *"The son has never seen his mother's final words before."* "Never seen before" twice in adjacent lines — the priming works (first-time/never-before field) but the doubled construction is a visible seam.

**Verdict on the link system:** mechanically flawless (placement, zero Thai, anchor presence) but semantically shallow in 4 of 8 slots. The pipeline is still priming *words*, not *fields* — it finds a sentence that contains the anchor token rather than building a beat that evokes the theme. The two good links (friend, remember) show the difference: there the beat is *about* the theme. Fix direction: the tag beat-pair should require a second theme-word (or a paraphrase of the theme) in the surrounding lines, and anchor selection should reject cognate collisions (careful/carefully) and stop-word-grade anchors (people, tell, before) where the tag's vocab allows a richer carrier.

## (b) The usual rubric

**Show don't tell — MIXED.** Strong concrete work: *"The ink on the envelope's address is smudged; Pom does not pick up the pen."*; the Funeral Timekeeper counting *"the hour with three taps, then seven, then three again"*; *"The paper does not burn."* But three bad tells, all at tag slots: *"Pom has something to tell Lek about the letter."*; *"Nobody would believe…"*; *"The silence between them is heavy with what hasn't been said."* (an abstraction; also note the irony — it precedes a tag about telling). Plus one artifact line: *"The pen-resting clerk is present."* (act 4) — a broken, machine-smelling sentence, almost certainly a residue of the machinery-stripping edit; it should have been caught by human or gate.

**Does the story make sense — MOSTLY.** The spine (letter must catch the tide before the pyre; Pom won't release it because the tone is wrong; Lek sends it anyway) is legible, and Pom's paralysis is a real, moving contradiction — the clerk whose spelling is perfect and whose letter never goes. Two coherence slips: (a) the Salt Clerk's *"Some things are better kept from people"* arrives unmotivated — nothing established that anyone wants anything hidden; (b) the re-arrival regression (below).

**Length — PASS.** 12 / 10 / 12 / 12 lines per act.

**Decisions — MIXED.** Choices: 10–20 words, concrete, staged, divergent outcomes — holding. Dilemma lines: **all four are the identical template** — *"Do I press Pom to seal the letter now, or take the unfinished letter to the Boatman myself?"* / *"Do I challenge…, or seek…?"* / *"Do I use…, or place…?"* / *"Do I ask…, or remain silent…?"* The new gate (reject bare "X or Y" <12 words with no "?") was routed around by length and a question mark: these are still menu-text, now in the PC's mouth, four times, same syntax. The PC narrating his own choice menu is a superposition-voice violation in spirit — "either/or" belongs in the choices, not spoken aloud.

**Outcomes — NEW TIC.** 23 of 24 outcomes begin *"I see…"* (one *"I feel…"*). The outcome-voice rule (begin with "I ") was satisfied by collapsing every consequence into passive perception: *"I see Pom's hand tighten on his pen."*, *"I see the paper absorb the scent and become rigid."* Individually fine; as a monoculture it flattens the PC's agency — he watches every consequence happen. The gate checks the first two characters; it cannot see that all 24 lines are the same sentence shape.

**Spatial — REGRESSED (new variant).** Act 2 segment 3: *"Lek and Pom enter the salt store"* — they have been in the salt store since act 2 segment 1. Act 3 segment 3: *"Lek and Pom arrive at the Wai Khru Mound"* — they have been at the mound all act (Lek holds out the envelope there in segment 1). Both look like the formatter/writer re-establishing place after a tag boundary. Rule 25 (every line true in the scene) violated twice.

**Introductions — REGRESSED.** The Boatman opens the episode with an ultimatum and no introduction; Pom is only ever "her quiet clerk"; the Caretaker and Funeral Timekeeper appear by role with no entrance beats. ep_005's opening-tableaux discipline has slipped.

**Deadline convergence — PASS.** Tide-turn planted act 1; act 3: *"the Boatman leaves now, with or without it."*

**Act 4 transformation — PASS.** The letter departs unresolved (tone never fixed; *"the world's turning edge a cut between knowing and not"* — purple, but the *shape* is right: urgency removed by departure, Pom's single written line left unread, no absolution).

**My three criteria:**
1. **Stakes legibility: 3/5.** Cost stated in line 1 (*"her son never knows"*) — but abstract; we never meet the son or learn why the letter matters beyond "final words." The pyre/tide double deadline helps.
2. **Choice–outcome consequentiality: 3/5 (was 4/5).** Outcomes diverge and are staged, but the "I see…" monoculture makes every consequence observational; the PC's *doing* vanishes between choice and result.
3. **Ensemble choreography: 2/5 (was 4/5).** Two re-arrivals at the current location, no introductions for three speaking characters, and the artifact line. Regression.

## (d) Bottom line for the PO

**On the validity side: yes — high but imperfect, good enough to move on.** All gates pass on the shipped artifact; segments, counts, places, outcomes, zero-Thai, and tag placement are all in contract; the episode is coherent and has genuine moments (the unburning paper, the three-taps-seven-taps counting). Nothing in this artifact would embarrass a player or break the frontend. The imperfect 20% is concentrated in three instruction-level classes — tag links that prime tokens not fields, menu-voice dilemmas, "I see…" outcome monoculture — none of which invalidates episodes already generated, and all of which are the same *kind* of problem: gates that check surface compliance get surface compliance. The recurring meta-lesson for the programmer: every first-token/presence gate needs a diversity or semantic companion check (dilemma-line syntax entropy across the 4 acts; outcome-verb diversity; anchor-collision rejection; second theme-word in tag beats).

**Recommended during-bulk watch items (updated from ep_005 regimen):** (1) per tag, does a *theme* appear in the 2–3 lines before the marker, not just the anchor token; (2) dilemma-line syntax diversity; (3) outcome first-verb diversity; (4) segment-boundary re-arrivals; (5) artifact-lint ("is present", dangling appositives). The pass-2 machinery rerun is the real open question — if machinery survives the gates, this pipeline is done at validity level.

## Headwinds / tailwinds

**Tailwinds:** First all-gates-PASS ship. The English-only tag pivot removed the entire unstageable-phrase problem class at a stroke, and the two good links ("friend", "remember") prove the writer *can* prime fields when the anchor is a real theme word. The Funeral Timekeeper's 3-7-3 tapping and the paper that does not burn are the kind of concrete, slightly numinous material the skill wants. The planner-picks-8-from-12 shortlist is the right structural answer to tag balancing. Format stage: 0 errors with deterministic repositioning.

**Headwinds:** The machinery strip is a warning about the keep-better guard's blindness: it optimizes problem *count*, and machinery beats are problem-*free* — so an edit that deletes them always looks safe. Until the guard weighs labeled-beat survival, any future gate wave can amputate the literary layer again. The compliance-surface pattern is now the dominant defect generator: dilemma lines, outcomes, and tag links all pass their gates while failing their purpose, and each needs a diversity/semantic companion check rather than a stricter version of the same check. Introductions regressed when nobody was watching them — gates that are relaxed between passes let old defects walk back in, so the full gate suite needs to run on every pass, not only the newly-fixed classes.
