## Act 1 — The Wai Khru Mound at Dawn
PLACE: The Wai Khru Mound
The Boatman: "Give me the sealed letter by the turn of the tide, or it stays here and her son never knows."
NARRATOR: The ink on the envelope's address is smudged; Pom does not pick up the pen.
The Monk: "The widow's pyre will be lit at first light."
The Boatman: "I cannot wait; the tide turns."
NARRATOR: Lek looks at Pom, who stares at his pen. He remembers the widow asking him to be a friend to her quiet clerk.
The Boatman: "Convince your friend, or take the letter yourself." [[tag_065]]
NARRATOR: Lek picks up the envelope; the paper feels thin and brittle.
NARRATOR: Pom reaches out and places a finger on the envelope, stopping Lek. The Funeral Timekeeper counts the hour with three taps, then seven, then three again.
NARRATOR: Lek cannot remember her final instruction. [[tag_182]]
NARRATOR: Lek stands at the Wai Khru Mound, watching the canal; Pom sits at a low desk with a steel pen on an open notebook.
NARRATOR: The Funeral Timekeeper taps the frangipani tree, the Boatman holds a travel pass, and the Monk waits by a pillar.
NARRATOR: The Funeral Timekeeper's rhythmic tapping is the only sound. Lek strains to remember the widow's exact words about the letter's importance.

## Act 2 — The Salt Store
PLACE: The Salt Store
Pom: "A word can be perfect and still cut the reader."
Lek: "Why is the letter not ready?"
Pom: "The spelling is correct, but the tone is wrong."
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk slides the salt into a sack, then steps forward, eyeing the envelope.
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk looks from the envelope to Lek; his eyes say he doesn't want people finding out about a clerk's failure.
The Salt Clerk: "Some things are better kept from people." [[tag_151]]
NARRATOR: The Clerk sees the exact weight where the scale's brass arm hangs level, a stillness of white grains; his silent judgment hangs in the dry air as Lek watches Pom, shoulders slumped.
NARRATOR: Pom isn't careful with his words, and now the letter may never go. [[tag_284]]
NARRATOR: Lek and Pom enter the salt store; the air is thick and dry. The Salt Clerk weighs salt without speaking, writes a figure in red chalk.
NARRATOR: Pom stands by a brick bin and runs his fingers through the pink salt.

## Act 3 — The Wai Khru Mound
PLACE: The Wai Khru Mound
NARRATOR: The Fire Stoker tends a low brazier, preparing incense for the funeral. The first hint of sun bleeds at the horizon.
NARRATOR: The Caretaker offers a ceremonial pen wrapped in silk; Pom declines with a shake of his head.
NARRATOR: Lek holds out the widow's envelope to Pom and tells him the Boatman leaves now, with or without it.
NARRATOR: The Fire Stoker feeds a stick of incense to the coals; a thin line of smoke rises straight up.
NARRATOR: Pom holds the envelope near the brazier. The Fire Stoker watches the paper, his eyes sharp.
The Fire Stoker: "Lift the letter carefully over the heat." [[tag_108]]
NARRATOR: Pom takes the envelope, walks to the brazier, and holds it over the heat. The paper does not burn.
NARRATOR: Pom turns and hands the envelope back to Lek. "You choose the heat."
NARRATOR: Nobody would believe a letter could be this difficult to send. [[tag_010]]
NARRATOR: Lek and Pom arrive at the Wai Khru Mound beneath the frangipani trees; the Caretaker sweeps fallen flowers into a pile.
NARRATOR: The thin line of incense smoke rises through the frangipani branches without bending, a straight pillar everyone ignores. Pom watches the unburning paper, his face the look of a man feeling a perfect cut.
NARRATOR: Pom looks at the straight smoke, then at the horizon. His expression is distant, seeing the widow from that day.

## Act 4 — The Departure
PLACE: The Salt Store
NARRATOR: The Monk chants softly, facing the direction of the widow's house.
NARRATOR: The Boatman holds out his hand. Lek gives him the envelope.
NARRATOR: The Boatman examines the address, now dry and crisp. He nods, tucks it inside his shirt, and pushes off into the canal.
NARRATOR: The Monk finishes his chant. Pom opens his notebook to a fresh page.
NARRATOR: Pom looks at Lek. The silence between them is heavy with what hasn't been said.
NARRATOR: Pom has something to tell Lek about the letter. [[tag_077]]
NARRATOR: He writes a single line, then closes the book. He places his steel pen on the cover. The pen-resting clerk is present.
NARRATOR: Lek watches the boat grow small. He thinks of the widow's son in Bangkok, a man he's never seen before.
NARRATOR: The son has never seen his mother's final words before. [[tag_006]]
NARRATOR: Lek and Pom return to the salt store.
NARRATOR: The first full light of dawn falls across the salt store, silent except for the water against the boat hull.
NARRATOR: The steel pen on the closed notebook holds the last of the ink that will never write that widow's tone again. The tide turns, the boat moves, the world's turning edge a cut between knowing and not.
