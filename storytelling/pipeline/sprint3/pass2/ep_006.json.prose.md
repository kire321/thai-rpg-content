## Act 1 — The Veranda Alarm
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: Lek stands on the veranda, watching the canal.
NARRATOR: Pom sits on the veranda bench, a notebook open on his lap, a pen in his hand.
NARRATOR: Wan leans against the veranda rail, holding a basket of folded cloth.
NARRATOR: The Monk kneels on a mat, a ceramic water bowl beside him.
NARRATOR: The Dance Teacher walks onto the veranda, her steps unsteady, and places a single frangipani blossom on the bench in front of Pom.
NARRATOR: “Correct the words or the ceremony dies.”
NARRATOR: The Dance Teacher whispers a phrase, her voice thin, struggling to remember the exact words. [[tag_182]]
NARRATOR: Pom’s pen stops moving over his notebook.
NARRATOR: He writes the phrase down.
NARRATOR: Bua slumps against the veranda post.
NARRATOR: Wan catches her before she falls.
NARRATOR: The Monk pours water into a bowl.
NARRATOR: Lek asks Pom what she said.
NARRATOR: Pom reads from his notebook: “Jai ron khaeng din.”
NARRATOR: The District Officer arrives by boat, his clerk carrying a leather folder.
NARRATOR: The District Officer steps onto the veranda, adjusting his uniform jacket.
NARRATOR: “What is this disturbance about?” the officer asks, his tone sharp, though his face attempts a friendly expression. [[tag_284]]
NARRATOR: Wan explains Bua fell ill.
NARRATOR: The officer sees Pom’s notebook open to the phrase.
NARRATOR: He laughs and calls it superstitious noise.
Pom: “It is not metaphor. It is measurement.”
NARRATOR: The officer’s smile vanishes.
NARRATOR: He declares the phrase incites unrest.
NARRATOR: He states the annual Wai Khru performance at the mound, which Bua leads, is hereby banned.
NARRATOR: Bua stirs and whispers, “The children have practiced for months.”
NARRATOR: The officer says a formal, written appeal may be submitted to his office.
NARRATOR: He says the appeal must be perfectly neutral in tone.
NARRATOR: He gives a deadline: the appeal must be delivered before the ceremony begins tomorrow evening.
NARRATOR: He leaves.
NARRATOR: The Monk helps Bua to her feet to take her home.
NARRATOR: Bua looks at Pom and then at Lek.
NARRATOR: “The words are not hot. The heart is. Write them cool.”
NARRATOR: She leaves with The Monk.
Wan: “The entire village expects the ceremony to happen.”
NARRATOR: Pom tears the page with the phrase from his notebook, folds it twice, and tucks it into his shirt pocket.
NARRATOR: He closes his notebook and looks at Lek.
Pom: “I will draft the appeal. You must choose which one to send.”

## Act 2 — The Salt Store Scales
PLACE: The Salt Store
NARRATOR: Lek and Pom walk to the salt store.
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk is weighing coarse grey salt into a cloth sack.
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk stands behind the counter, a brass scale in front of him.
NARRATOR: Pom sits on a brick ledge, opens a fresh page, and begins to write.
NARRATOR: Pom writes three separate sheets of paper.
NARRATOR: Lek watches the salt pour.
NARRATOR: Pom writes three separate sheets of paper.
NARRATOR: He lays them side by side on the ledge.
Pom: “This one uses only district office vocabulary. It mentions no phrase, no heart, no heat.”
Pom: “This one describes the cultural importance of the ceremony. It calls the phrase a traditional blessing.”
NARRATOR: He points to the third. It is blank.
Lek: “What is the blank page for?”
Pom: “A silent appeal. We submit nothing. We hold the ceremony anyway.”
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk finishes his work and watches them.
NARRATOR: “The pink salt is all gone. The officer’s men bought the last of it yesterday.”
NARRATOR: Pom gathers the three drafts, handling each sheet as if he has seen it before. [[tag_006]]
Pom: “Each version is a measurement. Each has a different temperature.”
NARRATOR: Pom holds the papers near the brazier, judging their warmth.
NARRATOR: “Which one should I prepare for final submission?” Pom asks Lek, his eyes bright with focus. [[tag_108]]
NARRATOR: The Salt Clerk adds that without the performance, the parents will not bring their rice chits for the school fund this season.
NARRATOR: Pom stands and walks to the store’s small brazier, used for melting resin.
NARRATOR: He holds the drafts near the coals.
Pom: “Choose. I will burn the other two.”

## Act 3 — The Mound's Incense
PLACE: The Wai Khru Mound
NARRATOR: At the Wai Khru mound, The Caretaker is sweeping dried frangipani leaves from the earth platform.
NARRATOR: Children are practicing a slow dance step under a tree.
NARRATOR: The Water Blessing Drummer tests the tension of his drum skin.
NARRATOR: The Caretaker sweeps the platform, a short broom in his hands.
NARRATOR: The Drummer sits on a stool, a long drum between his knees.
NARRATOR: Pom gives Lek the final, chosen appeal, written neatly on official paper.
NARRATOR: The Caretaker says the incense and betel offerings are ready.
NARRATOR: He asks if the permit has been granted.
NARRATOR: The District Officer’s boat is seen moving up the canal toward the mound.
NARRATOR: The children stop dancing and watch the boat.
NARRATOR: The officer steps onto the bank, his clerk behind him.
NARRATOR: He holds out his hand for the appeal.
NARRATOR: Lek hands him the paper.
NARRATOR: The officer reads it silently, his face unreadable, making Lek believe the appeal might fail. [[tag_010]]
NARRATOR: He looks from the paper to Pom, then to the children.
NARRATOR: “This is acceptable.”
NARRATOR: He folds the paper and puts it in his folder.
NARRATOR: He says the performance may proceed.
NARRATOR: He adds one condition: no one may speak the old phrase during the ceremony.
NARRATOR: Not in song, not in recitation.
NARRATOR: The drummer’s hand stills on his drum.
NARRATOR: The officer turns to leave, his back to the children, as if the people here no longer matter to him. [[tag_151]]
NARRATOR: One child asks if they can still sing the blessing song.
NARRATOR: “Sing whatever you like. Just do not say those words.”
NARRATOR: His boat pulls away.
NARRATOR: The Caretaker lights the incense.
NARRATOR: Pom walks to the edge of the mound, near the temple’s brazier for burning old offerings.
NARRATOR: He takes the two unused draft appeals from his pocket.
NARRATOR: He feeds them into the fire.
NARRATOR: He does not watch them burn.

## Act 4 — The Veranda Lamp
PLACE: The School Veranda
NARRATOR: Back on the veranda, the evening air carries the smell of incense from the mound.
NARRATOR: The Monk pours tea for Lek and Wan.
NARRATOR: Pom’s cup sits empty in front of him.
NARRATOR: The Caretaker arrives, holding a small ink cake, an offering from the ceremony.
NARRATOR: He gives it to Lek for the school.
NARRATOR: He says the ceremony was quiet, but complete.
Wan: “Did the children sing?”
NARRATOR: The Caretaker says they hummed the melody.
NARRATOR: No one spoke the words.
NARRATOR: Pom opens his notebook.
NARRATOR: He writes a single line.
NARRATOR: He closes the book.
Lek: “Is the appeal now part of the district record?”
Pom: “The words we sent are cold. The ones we kept are safe.”
NARRATOR: He takes the folded page from his shirt pocket, the original one with the phrase, holding it like a secret from a friend. [[tag_065]]
NARRATOR: He does not unfold it.
NARRATOR: He holds it over the veranda’s lamp.
NARRATOR: The edge of the paper browns and curls.
NARRATOR: He blows out the flame and puts the charred paper back in his pocket, his movements deliberate, as if to tell the page it is now safe. [[tag_077]]
NARRATOR: The Monk says, “Sometimes a silent heart is the loudest.”
NARRATOR: Pom picks up his pen and begins to write again, his hand moving steadily.
NARRATOR: The night sky holds the day's heat, the canal carries the moon's cool reflection, and the charred page in Pom's pocket is the exact warmth of a held breath.
NARRATOR: The veranda is quiet except for the sound of his pen and the distant beat of one drum from the mound.
