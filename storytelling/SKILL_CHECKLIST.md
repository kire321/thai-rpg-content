# DIALECTICAL FICTION — MASTER CHECKLIST (mandatory for all writing subagents; reviewers verify each item)

## Thesis machinery
1. The thesis is NEVER stated or discussed; no triadic dialectical shape; only its machinery appears on the page.
2. Noun phrases: object from domain A + descriptor from domain B, applied DIRECTLY as if obvious — no "like", "as if", "of" constructions, no category claims. (Good: "gone brackish"; bad: "his heart stuttered like some old machine".)
3. Opening line of the story/episode contains a thesis noun phrase.
4. The misfit-versus-setting tension is the FIRST and LAST thing the reader encounters (but is not the main plot).
5. Institutions: each a tight if/then logical implication of the thesis; shown through characters encountering/reacting to them, never narrator exposition; prefer REAL institutions from research over invention.
6. Actions: thesis implications that characters USE or SUFFER inside the story (soft magical realism allowed). If only the reader notices, cut or convert.
7. Slight fictionalization at the naming level ("the Fifth Reign", "the change") — discovery, not exposition.

## Desire
8. Every character: a concrete GOAL with a universal DRIVE underneath, written down in the private plan; the drive is never stated in the text.
9. Desire appears ONLY through practice (compulsive ritual), inventory (kept lists/catalogues), and attention (what they notice unasked). Tin-box standard: "She wrote it in pencil: wings." — never "she measured everything by whether it brought the wings nearer."
10. Someone wants something within the first beats of EVERY scene; characters are introduced through a want.
11. The protagonist's/episode's central desire thread is touched by every scene.
12. Every character is a contradiction — one structural irony each (a hacker who can't access the matrix).
13. Money-desire folded into moral identity with a specific sacrificial object ("so my brother can ordain", not "remittances").
14. The powerless get vivid, self-centered fantasies and acts in pursuit of them; fatalism never erases agency.
15. NO character development. Characters end as what they always were; only the world's name for them changes. No reader-culture moral templates.
16. Gendered power relations from the source material kept intact.

## Wonder & fatalism
17. One wonder beat MINIMUM per scene, using: exact loving inventory (specialist taxonomy, scale+precision) / the numinous domesticated (marvel at the kitchen table, nobody gasps — composure IS the wonder) / connoisseur attention rendered / elegiac frame (foreknowledge of loss, deployed everywhere).
18. Similes only from the story's own material ("bare heels like a pair of moons"), never imported.
19. Fatalism is COSMIC (Oedipus/Achilles: "your actions trace out the shape of the universe, as they must"), never washerwomen's pragmatism; fused with wonder.
20. A REFRAIN whose meaning demonstrably changes at each occurrence; the final occurrence reaches cosmic scale.
21. Gene Wolfe rule: characters already treat as obvious what the reader slowly perceives; never stage the revelation the reader expects.

## Structure
22. Four acts: (1) introduce contradiction, setting, characters, sticky situation; (2) problem more urgent; (3) maximally urgent — converge deadlines; (4) problem utterly TRANSFORMED so it is no longer urgent; the contradiction is absorbed, not resolved.
23. A sticky situation animates ALL characters.
24. Pipe-flow scenes: each scene's outcome causes the next; test by reading last line → next first line; cut driftwood.
25. Sadim pacing: line 1 = thesis + protagonist; line 2 = location; then two more characters, one already wanting; then protagonist wants; then trouble. Central object of desire appears EARLY.
26. Open acts and key scenes with ONE sentence carrying character + thesis phrase + sticky situation.

## Moral ambiguity
27. Two intense, conflicting moral impulses, both felt at full strength; the story argues for neither.
28. At least one "bastard" — real, courteous, nourishing as well as predatory (the Prince pawning the wings for a Buick). A pure villain is a failure.
29. Guilt lives in the body ("she cannot raise the wai past her chin to her sister's photograph"). No confession, repentance, or absolution.

## Quality discipline
30. The five qualities (wonder, empathy, moral ambiguity, fatalism, irony) are INSPIRED, never named or discussed in the text. If flat, add a beat that produces it — never commentary.
31. Anti-AI discipline: no "not X but Y" habit; few similes; no em-dash chains; no narrator verdicts after rendered moments; no high-signal AI vocabulary.
